ACW1120 – Financial Accounting 1

Q1. Define an “asset” according to the Conceptual Framework and give two examples.
An asset is a present economic resource controlled by the entity as a result of past events. An economic resource is a right that has the potential to produce economic benefits.

Examples: Cash, accounts receivable, inventory, property, plant and equipment, patents.
Q2. What is the difference between the cash basis and the accrual basis of accounting?
Cash basis: Revenues and expenses are recognised only when cash is received or paid.
Accrual basis: Revenues are recognised when earned and expenses when incurred, regardless of cash timing. The Conceptual Framework requires the accrual basis for general-purpose financial statements because it provides a better depiction of financial performance and position.
Q3. Equipment cost RM60,000, residual value RM6,000, useful life 9 years. Calculate annual straight-line depreciation and write the year-end adjusting entry.
Depreciable amount = 60,000 – 6,000 = 54,000
Annual depreciation = 54,000 / 9 = RM6,000

BFW1001 – Foundations of Finance

Q1. Calculate the future value of RM15,000 invested for 6 years at 7% p.a. compounded annually.
FV = 15,000 × (1.07)6 = 15,000 × 1.5007 ≈ RM22,511
Q2. What is the decision rule for Net Present Value (NPV)?
Accept the project if NPV > 0 (the project is expected to add value).
Reject if NPV < 0.
When ranking mutually exclusive projects, choose the one with the highest positive NPV.
Q3. Explain the risk-return trade-off in one or two sentences.
Investors require higher expected returns to compensate for taking on higher risk. Low-risk investments (e.g., government bonds) offer lower expected returns; high-risk investments (e.g., growth shares) must offer higher expected returns to attract capital.

BTW1042 – Business Law

Q1. Is a price tag on a product in a supermarket an offer or an invitation to treat? Explain.
It is generally an invitation to treat. The customer makes the offer by taking the item to the cashier; the shop accepts the offer by accepting payment. This protects the shop from being bound if stock is limited or a pricing error occurs. (Pharmaceutical Society of Great Britain v Boots Cash Chemists principle is often cited.)
Q2. List the four elements that must be proved to establish the tort of negligence.
1. Duty of care owed by the defendant to the plaintiff
2. Breach of that duty (fell below the standard of the reasonable person / professional)
3. Causation (the breach caused the damage)
4. Damage that is not too remote

ECM1953 – Principles of Economics

Q1. What happens to equilibrium price and quantity when demand increases and supply remains constant?
Both equilibrium price and equilibrium quantity rise. The demand curve shifts rightward; the new intersection with the supply curve is at a higher price and higher quantity.
Q2. Calculate PED: Price rises from RM20 to RM25; quantity demanded falls from 400 to 300 units.
% change in P = (5/20) × 100 = 25%
% change in Qd = (–100/400) × 100 = –25%
PED = –25% / 25% = –1 (unit elastic)

ETW1001 – Introduction to Statistical Analysis

Q1. When is the median a better measure of central tendency than the mean?
When the data distribution is skewed or contains extreme outliers. The median is not affected by extreme values, while the mean is pulled toward them.
Q2. In a simple linear regression Ŷ = 10 + 2.5X, interpret the slope coefficient.
For every one-unit increase in X, the predicted value of Y increases by 2.5 units, on average, holding other factors constant.

MGW1010 – Introduction to Management

Q1. Briefly contrast Theory X and Theory Y (McGregor).
Theory X: Managers assume employees dislike work, avoid responsibility and must be coerced or controlled.
Theory Y: Managers assume employees can find work enjoyable, are self-motivated and can accept (even seek) responsibility. Theory Y is associated with more participative management styles.
Q2. What is the contingency approach to management?
The contingency approach argues that there is no single best way to manage. The most effective management practices depend on the situation (environment, technology, size, people, etc.). Managers must diagnose the situation and adapt accordingly.

ACW2220 – Management Accounting 1

Q1. Selling price RM80, variable cost RM50, fixed costs RM90,000. Calculate break-even point in units and in sales ringgit.
CM per unit = 80 – 50 = RM30
Break-even units = 90,000 / 30 = 3,000 units
Break-even sales = 3,000 × 80 = RM240,000
Q2. What is a relevant cost in decision-making?
A relevant cost is a future cost that differs between the alternatives being considered. Sunk costs (already incurred) and future costs that do not differ between alternatives are irrelevant and should be ignored.
