ACW1120 – Financial Accounting 1

A. Objective / MCQ

1. Which of the following is a fundamental qualitative characteristic of useful financial information according to the Conceptual Framework?
Answer: Relevance and Faithful Representation
The two fundamental qualitative characteristics are Relevance and Faithful Representation. Comparability, Verifiability, Timeliness and Understandability are enhancing qualitative characteristics.
2. The accounting equation is best expressed as:
Answer: Assets = Liabilities + Equity
3. Under the accrual basis of accounting, revenue is recognised when:
Answer: It is earned, regardless of when cash is received.
4. Which account is debited when a business pays rent in advance for the next 12 months?
Answer: Prepaid Rent (an asset)

B. Calculation Questions

5. Equipment was purchased for RM48,000 on 1 January. Residual value is estimated at RM6,000 and useful life is 7 years. Calculate the annual straight-line depreciation and prepare the year-end adjusting entry.
Depreciable amount = 48,000 − 6,000 = RM42,000
Annual depreciation = 42,000 ÷ 7 = RM6,000

Adjusting entry (31 Dec):
Dr Depreciation Expense     6,000
   Cr Accumulated Depreciation – Equipment     6,000
6. On 1 October a firm received RM9,000 cash for services to be performed evenly over the next 6 months. What adjusting entry is required on 31 December (year-end)?
3 months of service have been performed (Oct–Dec).
Revenue earned = 9,000 × 3/6 = RM4,500

Dr Unearned Revenue     4,500
   Cr Service Revenue     4,500
7. A business has the following balances: Assets RM120,000, Liabilities RM45,000. What is the equity?
Equity = Assets − Liabilities = 120,000 − 45,000 = RM75,000

C. Subjective / Short Essay

8. Explain the difference between the cash basis and the accrual basis of accounting. Why does the Conceptual Framework prefer the accrual basis?
Cash basis: Revenues and expenses are recognised only when cash is received or paid.
Accrual basis: Revenues are recognised when earned and expenses when incurred, irrespective of cash flows.

The Conceptual Framework prefers the accrual basis because it better reflects the entity’s financial performance and position by matching revenues with related expenses and by recognising assets and liabilities when rights and obligations arise. This provides more relevant and faithfully representative information for users.
9. Define an asset according to the Conceptual Framework and explain the recognition criteria.
An asset is a present economic resource controlled by the entity as a result of past events. An economic resource is a right that has the potential to produce economic benefits.

An item is recognised as an asset when it meets the definition and recognition provides useful information (relevant and faithfully representative). In practice this usually requires that the inflow of economic benefits is probable and the item can be measured reliably.

BFW1001 – Foundations of Finance

A. Objective / MCQ

1. The time value of money concept states that:
Answer: A ringgit today is worth more than a ringgit in the future because of earning potential, inflation and risk.
2. Which capital budgeting technique directly measures the expected increase in value from a project?
Answer: Net Present Value (NPV)

B. Calculation Questions

3. Calculate the future value of RM12,000 invested for 5 years at 8% p.a. compounded annually.
FV = 12,000 × (1.08)5 = 12,000 × 1.4693 = RM17,632
4. You will receive RM25,000 in 4 years. Discount rate is 9%. What is the present value?
PV = 25,000 / (1.09)4 = 25,000 / 1.4116 ≈ RM17,710
5. Project requires RM80,000 initial investment. Cash inflows: Y1 RM30,000; Y2 RM35,000; Y3 RM40,000. Required return 10%. Calculate NPV and decide.
PV inflows = 30k/1.1 + 35k/1.21 + 40k/1.331 = 27,273 + 28,926 + 30,053 = 86,252
NPV = 86,252 − 80,000 = +RM6,252 → Accept
6. Present value of an ordinary annuity of RM5,000 per year for 6 years at 7%.
PVA = 5,000 × [1 − (1.07)−6] / 0.07 ≈ 5,000 × 4.7665 = RM23,833

C. Subjective

7. Explain the risk–return trade-off and why it matters for investors and managers.
Higher expected returns are required to compensate for higher risk. Rational investors only accept extra risk if compensated with higher expected return. Managers must use higher discount rates for riskier projects; otherwise they may accept value-destroying projects or reject value-creating ones.

BTW1042 – Business Law

A. Objective / MCQ

1. A price displayed on a product in a self-service store is generally regarded as:
Answer: An invitation to treat
2. Which is NOT an essential element of a valid contract under Malaysian law (in most cases)?
Answer: Written form (most contracts need not be in writing)

B. Subjective / Problem

3. Using IRAC: Ali offers to sell his car to Ben for RM40,000. Ben replies “I accept, but only if you include the spare tyre.” Ali remains silent. Is there a contract?
Issue: Valid acceptance?
Rule: Acceptance must be absolute and unqualified. A conditional acceptance is a counter-offer that rejects the original offer.
Application: Ben introduced a new term → counter-offer. Ali’s silence ≠ acceptance.
Conclusion: No contract.
4. Explain the four elements of the tort of negligence with a professional-advice illustration.
1. Duty of care (e.g. accountant–client)
2. Breach of duty (below standard of reasonably competent professional)
3. Causation (breach caused the loss)
4. Damage not too remote (loss reasonably foreseeable)

Example: Negligent tax advice relied upon by client who then incurs penalties.
5. Compare sole proprietorship, partnership and private limited company on liability and separate legal personality.
Sole prop: No separate personality; unlimited liability.
Partnership: Generally no separate personality; unlimited joint & several liability.
Sdn Bhd: Separate legal entity (Companies Act 2016); limited liability.

ECM1953 – Principles of Economics

A. Objective / MCQ

1. If demand increases while supply is constant, equilibrium price and quantity will:
Answer: Both rise
2. A firm maximises profit where:
Answer: MR = MC

B. Calculation

3. Price rises from RM10 to RM12; quantity demanded falls from 200 to 160. Calculate PED.
%ΔP = 20%; %ΔQd = −20%; PED = −1 → unit elastic
4. Fixed costs RM20,000; VC per unit RM15; SP RM25. Break-even quantity?
CM = 10; BE = 20,000 / 10 = 2,000 units

C. Subjective

5. Using AD-AS logic, explain the short-run effect of an increase in government spending on output and the price level.
G ↑ → AD shifts right → higher equilibrium real output and higher price level in the short run (movement along SRAS).

ETW1001 – Introduction to Statistical Analysis

A. Objective / MCQ

1. Which measure of central tendency is least affected by extreme outliers?
Answer: Median
2. A Type I error occurs when:
Answer: We reject a true null hypothesis

B. Calculation

3. Data: 5, 7, 9, 11, 13. Calculate mean and sample SD.
Mean = 9; Sample variance = 10; Sample SD = √10 ≈ 3.16
4. Ŷ = 15 + 3.2X (X = advertising RM’000, Y = sales RM’000). Interpret slope and intercept.
Intercept 15: predicted sales RM15,000 when advertising = 0.
Slope 3.2: each extra RM1,000 of advertising is associated with RM3,200 higher sales on average.

C. Subjective

5. Explain the difference between a population parameter and a sample statistic. Why do we usually work with sample statistics?
Parameter = characteristic of the whole population (e.g. μ). Statistic = characteristic calculated from a sample (e.g. x̄). We use samples because it is usually impractical to measure the entire population; we then infer about the population using confidence intervals and hypothesis tests.

MGW1010 – Introduction to Management

A. Objective / MCQ

1. Managers who believe employees are self-motivated follow:
Answer: Theory Y (McGregor)
2. The contingency approach argues that:
Answer: There is no one best way; the best approach depends on the situation.

B. Subjective

3. Critically evaluate Scientific Management in modern knowledge work / remote teams.
Strengths: efficiency, measurement, standardisation. Weaknesses: mechanistic view of workers, ignores social/psychological needs, assumes one best way. In knowledge work and remote settings, autonomy, creativity and intrinsic motivation matter more; pure Taylorism often demotivates and is poorly suited to non-routine tasks.
4. Distinguish transactional and transformational leadership with brief examples.
Transactional: exchange-based (rewards for performance). Example: commission-only sales manager.
Transformational: inspires followers via vision, intellectual stimulation, individualised consideration. Example: CEO who mobilises the organisation around a sustainability vision.

ACW2220 – Management Accounting 1

A. Objective / MCQ

1. Contribution margin equals:
Answer: Sales − all variable costs
2. In a special-order decision with spare capacity, relevant costs are:
Answer: Incremental variable costs and any additional fixed costs caused by the order (ignore sunk and unchanged allocated fixed costs).

B. Calculation

3. SP RM90, VC RM54, FC RM108,000. Calculate CM per unit, BE units, BE sales, and units for target profit RM36,000.
CM = RM36
BE units = 3,000
BE sales = RM270,000
Target-profit units = 4,000
4. Spare capacity. Customer offers RM70 for 800 units. Normal SP RM95, VC RM55, FC unchanged. Accept?
Incremental profit = (70 − 55) × 800 = RM12,000 → Accept
5. Make cost: DM 18 + DL 12 + Var OH 7 + Allocated Fixed OH 9. Supplier quote RM35. Fixed OH continues. Make or buy?
Relevant make cost = 18+12+7 = RM37
Buy cost = RM35 → Buy (saves RM2/unit), assuming quality/reliability OK.

C. Subjective

6. Explain fixed vs variable costs and why the distinction matters for CVP and decisions.
Variable costs change in total with activity; fixed costs stay constant in total within the relevant range. CVP uses contribution margin (Sales − VC) to cover FC and profit. In decisions we focus on costs that differ between alternatives; unchanged fixed costs are often irrelevant.
