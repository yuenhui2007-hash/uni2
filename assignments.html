ACW1120 – Financial Accounting 1

Task 1 — Adjusting entries + effect on profit

At 31 Dec, before adjustments: prepaid insurance RM6,000 (12 months from 1 Oct); unearned service revenue RM9,000 (of which 4 months earned); accrued salaries RM2,400; equipment cost RM48,000, residual RM6,000, life 7 years, full year, straight-line. Prepare all adjusting entries and state the net effect on profit.

Workings
Insurance expired: 6,000 × 3/12 = 1,500
Revenue earned: 9,000 × 4/12 = 3,000 (or state months clearly if different fraction)
Depreciation: (48,000 − 6,000) / 7 = 6,000

Journals
Dr Insurance expense 1,500   Cr Prepaid insurance 1,500
Dr Unearned revenue 3,000   Cr Service revenue 3,000
Dr Salaries expense 2,400   Cr Salaries payable 2,400
Dr Depreciation expense 6,000   Cr Accumulated depreciation 6,000

Net effect on profit: −1,500 +3,000 −2,400 −6,000 = −6,900 (profit decreases by RM6,900)
Marks tip: Show fraction workings, never put Cash in adjustments, state profit effect with sign.

Task 2 — Conceptual Framework short essay (≈350–500 words)

Explain the objective of general-purpose financial reporting and the fundamental qualitative characteristics. Apply materiality to a scenario where a company omits a related-party fraud of RM80,000 while total assets are RM50 million.

Structure for high marks: define → explain → apply → conclude.

The objective of general-purpose financial reporting under the IASB Conceptual Framework is to provide financial information about the reporting entity that is useful to existing and potential investors, lenders and other creditors in making decisions about providing resources to the entity. Those decisions involve buying, selling or holding equity and debt instruments, and providing or settling loans and other forms of credit. Users also assess management’s stewardship of the entity’s economic resources.

Useful information must be relevant and faithfully represented. Relevance means the information has predictive and/or confirmatory value. Materiality is an entity-specific aspect of relevance: information is material if omitting, misstating or obscuring it could reasonably be expected to influence decisions of primary users. Faithful representation requires the depiction of economic substance to be as complete, neutral and free from error as practicable.

Application: Although RM80,000 is only 0.16% of total assets, the nature of the item (related-party fraud) is qualitatively material. Fraud indicates governance failure and affects users’ assessment of stewardship and the reliability of reported numbers. Omitting it could reasonably influence lenders’ and investors’ decisions. Therefore disclosure (and appropriate accounting) is required despite the small quantitative size.

Conclusion: Quantitative thresholds alone do not determine materiality; nature and context matter.
Marks tip: Quote Framework language accurately; always apply to the numbers/facts given; mention both quantitative and qualitative materiality.

Task 3 — Periodic inventory COGS

Opening inventory RM22,000; Purchases RM95,000; Purchase returns RM4,000; Freight-in RM3,500; Closing inventory RM19,500; Sales RM160,000; Sales returns RM5,000. Calculate net sales, COGS and gross profit.

Net sales = 160,000 − 5,000 = 155,000
Net purchases = 95,000 − 4,000 + 3,500 = 94,500
COGS = 22,000 + 94,500 − 19,500 = 97,000
Gross profit = 155,000 − 97,000 = 58,000
Marks tip: Label each subtotal; include freight-in in purchases cost; exclude sales returns from net sales.

BFW1001 – Foundations of Finance

Task 1 — NPV decision with full workings

Project cost RM120,000. Cash inflows: Y1 40,000; Y2 50,000; Y3 55,000. Required return 10%. Calculate NPV and recommend. Briefly explain why NPV is preferred to payback.

PV₁ = 40,000/1.10 = 36,364
PV₂ = 50,000/1.21 = 41,322
PV₃ = 55,000/1.331 = 41,322
Σ PV inflows = 119,008
NPV = 119,008 − 120,000 = −992 ≈ −RM1,000 → REJECT

NPV is preferred to payback because it incorporates the time value of money for all cash flows over the full life of the project and measures the expected change in wealth. Payback ignores cash flows after the cutoff and (unless discounted) ignores TVM, so it is only a liquidity screen, not a value-maximising rule.
Marks tip: Show each PV line; state Accept/Reject with NPV figure; give one clear conceptual reason for NPV over payback.

Task 2 — Bond price

Face RM1,000; annual coupon 5% (RM50); 4 years to maturity; market yield 7%. Calculate the bond price and state whether it trades at premium, par or discount.

Price = 50/1.07 + 50/1.07² + 50/1.07³ + 1,050/1.07⁴
= 46.73 + 43.67 + 40.81 + 816.30 ≈ RM947.51
Coupon 5% < yield 7% → bond trades at a discount.
Marks tip: Include principal in final CF; link coupon vs yield to premium/discount.

BTW1042 – Business Law

Task 1 — Contract IRAC (counter-offer)

Rina offers to sell her laptop to Sam for RM2,500. Sam emails: “I accept, provided you include the original charger.” Rina does not reply. Sam claims a contract exists. Advise using IRAC.

Issue: Whether a binding contract was formed between Rina and Sam.

Rule: A contract requires offer and unqualified acceptance. Acceptance must mirror the terms of the offer. A purported acceptance that introduces new terms is a counter-offer, which rejects the original offer (Contracts Act 1950 principles / common-law mirror-image rule as applied in Malaysia). Silence does not generally constitute acceptance.

Application: Sam’s reply added a new term (the charger). This was not an unqualified acceptance but a counter-offer, which rejected Rina’s original offer. Rina’s silence cannot be treated as acceptance of Sam’s counter-offer.

Conclusion: No contract was formed. Sam’s claim fails.
Marks tip: Full IRAC labels; state the mirror-image / counter-offer rule before applying facts; clear final advice.

Task 2 — Sdn Bhd vs partnership (structure advice)

Two graduates want to start a consulting firm. They worry about personal liability and may seek investors later. Advise between partnership and Sdn Bhd under Malaysian law.

Partnership (Partnership Act 1961): Generally no separate legal personality; partners have unlimited joint and several liability for firm debts. Continuity is fragile on change of partners. Raising outside equity is awkward.

Sdn Bhd (Companies Act 2016): Separate legal entity; members’ liability limited to unpaid shares; perpetual succession; can issue shares to investors (subject to private-company limits: max 50 members, no public offer). Requires ≥1 resident director, company secretary, and ongoing SSM compliance.

Advice: Given liability concern and possible future investors, Sdn Bhd is preferable. Limited liability protects personal assets (subject to veil-piercing exceptions and personal guarantees banks may still demand). Compliance cost is higher than a partnership but aligned with growth and risk isolation.
Marks tip: Compare on personality, liability, continuity, capital; end with a reasoned recommendation tied to the clients’ goals.

ACW2220 – Management Accounting 1

Task 1 — Special order decision

Normal SP RM80, VC RM48, annual FC RM320,000. Capacity 20,000 units; current sales 15,000. Customer offers RM58 for 3,000 units; no extra FC. Accept? Show incremental analysis. What if capacity were full?

Spare capacity (5,000 idle > 3,000):
Incremental revenue = 58 × 3,000 = 174,000
Incremental VC = 48 × 3,000 = 144,000
Incremental profit = 30,000 → ACCEPT
(Regular FC unchanged → irrelevant)

If full capacity: must displace 3,000 regular units.
Forgone CM on regular = (80−48)×3,000 = 96,000
CM on special = (58−48)×3,000 = 30,000
Net = 30,000 − 96,000 = −66,000 → REJECT
Marks tip: Always state capacity assumption; opportunity cost line when constrained; ignore sunk/unchanged FC.

Task 2 — CVP target profit

SP RM50, VC RM30, FC RM100,000. Units for target profit RM40,000? Margin of safety if expected sales 9,000 units?

CM = 20
Target units = (100,000 + 40,000) / 20 = 7,000 units
BE units = 100,000 / 20 = 5,000
MoS = 9,000 − 5,000 = 4,000 units (44.4% of expected sales)

ECM1953 – Principles of Economics

Task 1 — PED + pricing advice

Price rises from RM20 to RM24; quantity demanded falls from 1,000 to 900. Calculate PED (use midpoint if you wish) and advise whether a further price increase raises or lowers total revenue.

%ΔP = 4/20 = 20% (simple) or midpoint: 4/22 ≈ 18.2%
%ΔQd = −100/1,000 = −10% (simple) or midpoint: −100/950 ≈ −10.5%
PED ≈ −10/20 = −0.5 (inelastic)

Because |PED| < 1, a further price increase should raise total revenue (percentage fall in Qd smaller than percentage rise in P).
Marks tip: Show formula and arithmetic; link |PED| to TR direction explicitly.

Task 2 — Fiscal policy AD–AS brief

Government increases infrastructure spending. Explain short-run effects on AD, real output and the price level. One limitation?

Higher G raises planned expenditure → AD shifts right. In the short run, the economy moves along SRAS to a higher real output (Y) and a higher price level (P).

Limitation: if the economy is near capacity, the output gain is small and inflationary pressure is stronger; financing via borrowing may also raise interest rates and crowd out private investment (partial offset to AD).
Marks tip: Name the curve shift; state both Y and P effects; add one realistic limitation for higher-band answers.

ETW1001 – Statistical Analysis

Task 1 — Mean, variance, interpretation

Data: 12, 15, 18, 20, 25. Compute mean and sample SD. Why might median be preferred if one value were 80 instead of 25?

Mean = (12+15+18+20+25)/5 = 18
Deviations²: 36+9+0+4+49 = 98
s² = 98/(5−1) = 24.5 → s = √24.5 ≈ 4.95

If 25 became 80, the mean would be pulled upward by the outlier while the median (18) would stay stable. Median better represents “typical” value under skewness/outliers.

Task 2 — Regression interpretation

Ŷ = 10 + 2.5X (X = training hours, Y = productivity score), R² = 0.55. Interpret slope and R². Can you claim training causes higher productivity?

Slope 2.5: each additional training hour is associated with a 2.5-point higher predicted productivity score, on average.
R² 0.55: 55% of the sample variation in productivity scores is explained by training hours in this linear model.

Causality: No — observational regression shows association, not causation, unless the study design supports causal inference (e.g. random assignment, credible controls). Omitted factors (ability, prior experience) may confound.
Marks tip: Interpret in context units; explicitly refuse unjustified causal claims.

MGW1010 – Introduction to Management

Task 1 — Critically evaluate Scientific Management (essay skeleton + sample paragraphs)

Critically evaluate Taylor’s Scientific Management for a software development team working remotely.

High-band structure: Define → Explain principles → Apply to remote software teams → Critique → Conclude with implication.

Definition/explain: Scientific Management (Taylor) seeks efficiency through scientific study of tasks, standard methods, careful selection and training, and financial incentives linked to output.

Application: Some elements still appear in software work (sprint metrics, code review standards, pair programming norms). Time-and-motion style measurement maps poorly onto creative design and debugging, where output is non-linear and quality matters more than keystroke counts.

Critique: Taylorism assumes workers are primarily economically motivated and tasks are routine and decomposable. Remote knowledge work depends on autonomy, intrinsic motivation, collaboration and psychological safety (Theory Y / job characteristics). Heavy surveillance of activity metrics can reduce trust and citizenship behaviour. Contingency theory suggests mechanistic controls fit stable, routine contexts — not dynamic product development.

Conclusion: Retain process discipline and clear standards where useful, but reject pure Taylorism; combine agile coordination with autonomy-supportive leadership for remote software teams.
Marks tip: Critique is mandatory for upper bands — assumptions, evidence, modern fit. End with managerial implication.
