ACW2220 – Management Accounting 1

Degree-level: cost behaviour, CVP assumptions, relevant costing frameworks, decision pitfalls

1. Purpose of Management Accounting

Management accounting supports internal planning, control and decision-making. Unlike financial accounting, it is not bound by external reporting standards; relevance and cost-benefit of information dominate. Product costs, customer costs and segment reports may use different cost objects and allocation bases depending on the decision. At degree level you must state the decision context before selecting which costs matter.

2. Cost Taxonomy for Analysis

Behaviour

  • Variable: total varies with activity; unit variable cost approximately constant in the relevant range
  • Fixed: total constant in the relevant range; unit fixed cost declines with volume
  • Mixed: fixed plus variable component; separate for CVP using high-low, scattergraph or regression

The relevant range is the band of activity over which the cost-behaviour assumptions remain valid. Outside that range, step-fixed costs or non-linear variable costs appear — a frequent essay point.

Decision relevance

  • Relevant costs/revenues: future amounts that differ among alternatives
  • Sunk costs: past, irreversible — irrelevant to the choice (though they may affect accounting profit)
  • Opportunity cost: benefit of the best forgone alternative — often relevant and often omitted from the ledger

3. CVP Analysis — Structure and Limitations

Contribution margin (CM) = Sales − Variable costs. CM first covers fixed costs; residual is operating profit. CM ratio = CM / Sales.
Break-even units = FC / CM per unit
Break-even sales = FC / CM ratio
Target-profit units = (FC + Target profit) / CM per unit
Margin of safety = Expected sales − Break-even sales (in units or RM; can be expressed as % of expected sales)

Assumptions (must be stated in higher-mark answers): constant selling price and unit variable cost; linear total cost; constant sales mix (multi-product); production = sales (no inventory change); costs separable into fixed and variable within the relevant range.

SP 90, VC 54, FC 108,000 → CM = 36
BE = 3,000 units (sales 270,000). Target profit 36,000 → 4,000 units.
If expected sales = 5,000 units, margin of safety = 2,000 units (40% of expected volume).

4. Relevant Costing — Decision Protocols

Method: identify alternatives → isolate differential cash flows → ignore sunk and non-differential allocated costs → incorporate opportunity costs of scarce resources → recommend, noting qualitative factors (capacity, quality, strategy, risk, long-term pricing effects).

Special order

With idle capacity, fixed costs often unchanged: accept if special price covers incremental variable costs plus any extra fixed costs and yields positive contribution. If capacity is constrained, include opportunity cost of displaced regular contribution.

Make or buy

Relevant make cost = avoidable manufacturing costs (DM, DL, variable OH, avoidable fixed) + opportunity cost of capacity. Compare with purchase price. Continuing allocated fixed overhead is irrelevant. Assess supplier reliability and strategic control of know-how.

Keep or drop a segment

Compare CM lost with fixed costs avoided. Allocated common fixed costs that remain after the drop must not be treated as “saved”. Dropping can reduce profit if avoidable fixed costs < CM forgone.

Segment CM 80,000; avoidable FC 50,000; common allocated FC 40,000 (continues).
Dropping loses 80,000 CM, saves only 50,000 → worse by 30,000 → keep.

5. Product Costing Systems (Exam Awareness)

  • Job costing: costs traced to discrete jobs (custom production/services)
  • Process costing: average costs over homogeneous continuous output
  • ABC: multiple activity drivers for overhead when products consume activities disproportionately — improves cost accuracy for pricing/mix decisions but is costlier to operate
Exam technique: Always state what is relevant and why. Present a clear incremental analysis table. Conclude with a recommendation plus one qualitative caveat. Marks are lost when sunk or continuing allocated costs are treated as differential.
