1. Management vs financial accounting

Financial accounting → external users, standards, historical. Management accounting → internal managers, future-oriented, no GAAP constraint, cost-benefit of information. Same organisation; different questions (“What is profit under IFRS?” vs “Should we accept this order?”).

Relevant range: activity band where cost-behaviour assumptions hold. Outside it, step-fixed costs appear.

Sunk cost: already incurred, irreversible → ignore for forward decisions.
Opportunity cost: benefit of best alternative forgone → often relevant, often not in the ledger.

3. CVP analysis

Contribution margin = Sales − All variable costs. CM covers fixed costs then profit.
CM per unit = p − v
CM ratio = CM / Sales
Break-even units = FC / CM per unit
Break-even sales = FC / CM ratio
Target-profit units = (FC + Target profit) / CM per unit
Margin of safety = Expected sales − BE sales

Assumptions to state: constant p and v; linear costs; constant mix; sales = production; relevant range holds.

p=90, v=54, FC=108,000, target profit=36,000
CM=36 → BE=3,000 units (sales 270,000)
Target units=(108,000+36,000)/36=4,000
If expected sales=5,000, MoS=2,000 units (40%)

4. Relevant costing decisions

Protocol: (1) alternatives (2) differential future cash flows (3) opportunity costs (4) ignore sunk & non-differential allocated FC (5) qualitative factors.

Special order (spare capacity)

Normal p=95, v=55. Offer p=70 for 800 units. FC unchanged. Spare capacity.
Incremental profit = (70−55)×800 = 12,000 → ACCEPT
If full capacity and displaces regular sales, subtract forgone CM on lost units.

Make or buy

Make: DM18 + DL12 + Var OH7 + Allocated fixed OH9 = 46 total accounting
Relevant make = 18+12+7 = 37 (fixed continues)
Buy quote = 35 → BUY saves 2/unit if quality OK

Keep or drop

CM 80,000; avoidable FC 50,000; allocated common FC 40,000 continues.
Dropping: lose 80k CM, save only 50k → worse by 30k → KEEP

Official overview: cost information for planning, control and decision-making. Topics include costs and cost behaviour, product costing, cost allocation techniques, CVP, and use of cost information for management decisions. 8. Product costing systems

Job costing: costs traced to distinct jobs (custom work). DM + DL traced; OH applied via predetermined rate.

Predetermined OH rate = Estimated total OH / Estimated allocation base
Applied OH = Rate × Actual base used by job

Process costing: homogeneous continuous production; average cost per equivalent unit.

Under/over applied OH: Applied − Actual. Closing treatment: write off to COGS or prorate (as taught).

Estimated OH 240,000; estimated DL hours 60,000 → rate = RM4 per DLH
Job uses 800 DLH → applied OH = 3,200
If actual OH for period 250,000 and total applied 245,000 → under-applied 5,000

9. Cost allocation and ABC insight

Volume-based allocation (DLH, MH) can distort product costs when products differ in batch size, complexity, or support-activity use. Activity-based costing (ABC) uses multiple activity cost pools and drivers (setups, inspections, orders). Better for pricing/mix decisions when overhead is large and diversity is high; costlier to maintain.

10. Absorption vs variable costing (if in unit)

Absorption: fixed manufacturing OH in product cost → inventory carries fixed OH. Variable: fixed manufacturing OH treated as period cost. Profit differs when production ≠ sales (inventory change).
