ACW2220 – Management Accounting 1

Deep concepts: cost behaviour, CVP, relevant costing, decisions

1. Management Accounting vs Financial Accounting

Financial accounting reports to external users under standards and is largely historical. Management accounting serves internal managers for planning, control and decision-making. It is future-oriented, not bound by external GAAP/IFRS, and emphasises relevance and timeliness over strict uniformity. Cost information is a central input.

2. Cost Concepts – Going Deeper

By behaviour

  • Variable costs – Total changes in proportion to activity; cost per unit is constant within the relevant range (e.g. direct materials).
  • Fixed costs – Total stays constant within the relevant range; cost per unit falls as activity rises (e.g. factory rent). “Relevant range” is the band of activity where the cost behaviour assumption holds.
  • Mixed costs – Part fixed, part variable; often separated using high-low method or regression for analysis.

By traceability

  • Direct costs – Can be traced economically to a cost object (product, department, customer).
  • Indirect costs – Cannot be traced conveniently; must be allocated using a cost driver.

By decision relevance

  • Relevant costs – Future costs that differ between alternatives
  • Sunk costs – Already incurred; cannot be changed; irrelevant to forward-looking decisions
  • Opportunity cost – Benefit forgone by choosing one alternative over another; often relevant even if not in the accounting records

3. Cost-Volume-Profit (CVP) – Conceptual Framework

Contribution margin (CM) = Sales − All variable costs. CM is the amount available to cover fixed costs and then provide profit. CM ratio = CM / Sales.

CVP analysis studies how profit changes with selling price, volume, variable cost and fixed cost. It rests on simplifying assumptions: constant SP and unit VC, linear costs, constant sales mix (if multi-product), and no inventory change (production = sales).

Break-even units = Fixed costs / CM per unit
Break-even sales = Fixed costs / CM ratio
Units for target profit = (Fixed costs + Target profit) / CM per unit
Margin of safety = Actual (or budgeted) sales − Break-even sales

Margin of safety measures how far sales can fall before the firm makes a loss — a useful risk indicator.

SP 90, VC 54, FC 108,000 → CM = 36
BE = 108,000/36 = 3,000 units (sales RM270,000)
For profit 36,000 → units = 144,000/36 = 4,000

4. Relevant Costing – Decision Framework

The correct approach to short-term decisions is: identify alternatives → identify which cash flows change → ignore sunk and non-differential items → choose the alternative that maximises incremental benefit (or minimises incremental cost), subject to qualitative factors (quality, capacity, strategy, risk).

Special order (with spare capacity)

If capacity is idle, fixed costs usually do not change. Accept the order if the special price covers incremental variable costs (and any extra fixed costs) and leaves a positive contribution. If the order displaces regular sales, include the opportunity cost of lost contribution.

Make or buy

Compare relevant cost to make (DM + DL + variable OH + avoidable fixed costs + opportunity cost of capacity) with the purchase price. Allocated fixed overhead that continues either way is irrelevant. Consider quality, reliability of supply and strategic control.

Keep or drop a segment

Compare contribution margin lost if the segment is dropped with the fixed costs that would actually be avoided. Allocated common fixed costs that remain after the drop should not be charged against the segment when deciding.

5. Product Costing Systems (Overview)

  • Job costing – Costs tracked by individual job (custom work)
  • Process costing – Costs averaged over homogeneous units in continuous processes
  • Activity-based costing (ABC) – Overhead assigned via multiple activity cost drivers for more accurate product/customer costs when products consume activities differently

Choice of system affects reported product cost and can influence pricing and product-mix decisions; managers should understand the assumptions behind the numbers.

Exam strategy: For every decision question, explicitly list what changes and what does not. Show a clear incremental analysis. End with a recommendation and one qualitative caveat (e.g. capacity, quality, long-term customer relationship).
