1. Management vs financial accounting

Financial accounting
  • External users (investors, lenders)
  • Governed by standards / GAAP / IFRS
  • Historical focus
  • General-purpose reports
Management accounting
  • Internal managers
  • No GAAP constraint
  • Future-oriented
  • Cost–benefit of information matters
Same organisation, different questions: “What is profit under IFRS?” vs “Should we accept this order?”

3. CVP analysis

Core techniqueAlways write CM per unit first. State assumptions. End with a decision sentence.
Contribution margin = Sales − All variable costs. CM covers fixed costs then profit.
CM per unit = p − v
CM ratio = CM / Sales
Break-even units = FC / CM per unit
Break-even sales = FC / CM ratio
Target-profit units = (FC + Target profit) / CM per unit
Margin of safety = Expected sales − BE sales

Assumptions to state: constant p and v; linear costs; constant mix; sales = production; relevant range holds.

p=90, v=54, FC=108,000, target profit=36,000
CM=36 → BE=3,000 units (sales 270,000)
Target units=(108,000+36,000)/36=4,000
If expected sales=5,000, MoS=2,000 units (40%)

4. Relevant costing decisions

  1. List the alternatives clearly.
  2. Identify differential future cash flows only.
  3. Add opportunity costs if capacity is constrained.
  4. Ignore sunk costs and allocated fixed costs that do not change.
  5. Note qualitative factors (quality, staff, customer relationships).
Do notUse full absorption cost per unit alone to accept/reject a short-term special order — it can include fixed overhead that will not change.

Special order (spare capacity)

Normal p=95, v=55. Offer p=70 for 800 units. FC unchanged. Spare capacity.
Incremental profit = (70−55)×800 = 12,000 → ACCEPT
If full capacity and displaces regular sales, subtract forgone CM on lost units.

Make or buy

Make: DM18 + DL12 + Var OH7 + Allocated fixed OH9 = 46 total accounting
Relevant make = 18+12+7 = 37 (fixed continues)
Buy quote = 35 → BUY saves 2/unit if quality OK

Keep or drop

CM 80,000; avoidable FC 50,000; allocated common FC 40,000 continues.
Dropping: lose 80k CM, save only 50k → worse by 30k → KEEP

8. Product costing systems

Job costing: costs traced to distinct jobs (custom work). DM + DL traced; OH applied via predetermined rate.

Predetermined OH rate = Estimated total OH / Estimated allocation base
Applied OH = Rate × Actual base used by job

Process costing: homogeneous continuous production; average cost per equivalent unit.

Under/over applied OH: Applied − Actual. Closing treatment: write off to COGS or prorate (as taught).

Estimated OH 240,000; estimated DL hours 60,000 → rate = RM4 per DLH
Job uses 800 DLH → applied OH = 3,200
If actual OH for period 250,000 and total applied 245,000 → under-applied 5,000

9. Cost allocation and ABC insight

Volume-based allocation (DLH, MH) can distort product costs when products differ in batch size, complexity, or support-activity use. Activity-based costing (ABC) uses multiple activity cost pools and drivers (setups, inspections, orders). Better for pricing/mix decisions when overhead is large and diversity is high; costlier to maintain.

10. Absorption vs variable costing (if in unit)

Absorption: fixed manufacturing OH in product cost → inventory carries fixed OH. Variable: fixed manufacturing OH treated as period cost. Profit differs when production ≠ sales (inventory change).

12. High-low cost estimation

Variable cost per unit = (Cost at highest activity − Cost at lowest activity) / (High activity − Low activity)
Fixed cost = Total cost at high point − (VC per unit × high activity)
High: 10,000 units, cost 92,000
Low: 6,000 units, cost 68,000
VC = (92,000−68,000)/(10,000−6,000) = 24,000/4,000 = RM6 per unit
FC = 92,000 − 6×10,000 = 32,000
Cost equation: Y = 32,000 + 6X
Do notUse high-low outside the observed activity range without caution — linearity may fail.

13. Multi-product CVP (constant mix)

Products A and B sold in mix 2:1. CMA=20, CMB=30. FC=140,000.
CM per pack (2A+1B) = 40+30 = 70
BE packs = 140,000/70 = 2,000 packs → A 4,000 units, B 2,000 units
If mix changes, weighted CM changes and BE shifts. Always state the mix assumption.

14. Special order — full capacity detailed

Capacity 8,000; current sales 8,000 (full). SP 100, VC 55, FC 200,000.
Order: 1,000 units at 80; no extra FC.
CM regular lost = (100−55)×1,000 = 45,000
CM order = (80−55)×1,000 = 25,000
Net = 25,000 − 45,000 = −20,000 → REJECT

15. Keep/drop with opportunity reuse

Segment CM 90,000; avoidable FC 40,000; allocated common FC 25,000 continues.
If dropped and capacity earns alternative CM 30,000:
Effect of drop = −90,000 +40,000 +30,000 = −20,000 → still worse → KEEP
(Without alternative use: −90,000+40,000=−50,000)

16. Job costing — full flow

  1. Trace DM and DL to job.
  2. Apply OH: predetermined rate × actual base on job.
  3. Job cost = DM + DL + applied OH.
  4. On completion: WIP → FG; on sale: FG → COGS.
  5. Under/over applied OH closed as taught (to COGS or prorate).
Rate = 180,000 budgeted OH / 30,000 DLH = RM6/DLH
Job 21: DM 8,000; DL 5,000; 400 DLH → OH applied 2,400
Job cost = 15,400

17. Throughput thinking under a bottleneck

When a scarce resource constrains output, rank products by CM per scarce resource unit (e.g. per machine hour), not by CM per unit alone.

Product A: CM 30, uses 2 hours → CM/hour 15
Product B: CM 40, uses 4 hours → CM/hour 10
Scarce hours → prioritise A
