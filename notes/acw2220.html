ACW2220 – Management Accounting 1

Advanced notes: cost behaviour modelling, CVP under constraints, relevant-cost decision protocols, common exam traps

1. Decision-Led Cost Analysis

Management accounting designs cost objects (product, job, customer, channel, segment) to answer specific decisions. Different decisions require different cost aggregations — full absorption cost for inventory valuation under financial reporting is often the wrong input for short-term pricing or make-or-buy. The degree skill is selecting the cost construction that matches the decision horizon and constraint set.

2. Cost Behaviour and the Relevant Range

Variable costs are linear in activity only inside the relevant range; quantity discounts, overtime premiums and step-fixed supervision create piecewise-linear or nonlinear total cost functions. High-low estimates use only two points and are outlier-sensitive; least-squares regression uses all points and yields R²/standard error for fit assessment when required.

Committed vs discretionary fixed costs matter for keep-or-drop: only avoidable fixed costs enter the differential analysis.

3. CVP — Algebra, Multi-Product and Limitations

QBE = FC / (p − v)
SalesBE = FC / CM ratio
Qtarget = (FC + π) / (p − v)
Margin of safety % = (Qexpected − QBE) / Qexpected

Operating leverage: high FC / low VC amplifies profit sensitivity to volume (degree of operating leverage ≈ CM / operating profit at a given volume). Multi-product CVP requires a constant sales mix; break-even in “packages” of the mix, then decompose into units of each product. Inventory change violates the sales = production assumption and confounds contribution analysis with absorption costing fixed-overhead volume variances (bridge to later topics).

4. Relevant Costing — Formal Protocol

  1. Define alternatives and the decision horizon
  2. List all future cash flows that differ
  3. Include opportunity costs of scarce resources (contribution displaced)
  4. Exclude sunk costs and non-differential allocated overhead
  5. Optimise quantitative criterion then stress-test qualitative factors (quality, dependence on supplier, strategic option value, customer retaliation on segmented pricing)

Special orders

Idle capacity: minimum acceptable price = unit variable cost + unitised extra fixed costs + any unit opportunity cost. Full capacity: opportunity cost = forgone CM on regular sales displaced. Predatory-pricing / long-term customer reference-price effects are qualitative overlays.

Make vs buy

Relevant make cost = avoidable costs + opportunity cost of freed capacity (highest-valued alternative use). Allocated HQ overhead that continues is irrelevant. Strategic factors: IP leakage, supply-chain resilience, core competence.

Segment continuity

CM 120,000; avoidable FC 70,000; unavoidable allocated FC 55,000.
Segment margin on avoidable basis = 50,000 > 0 → keep.
If the examiner allocates 55,000 and shows an “accounting loss”, the trap is treating unavoidable FC as saved by dropping.

5. Costing Systems and Distorted Decisions

Volume-based overhead allocation cross-subsidises low-volume complex products when overhead is driven by batches, setups, design hours (ABC insight). Degree exams may require narrative critique of unit-cost figures used for pricing when the allocation base is weak.

High-band answers: explicit relevant-cost schedule; opportunity cost line item; qualitative paragraph; sensitivity (“if capacity were constrained…”). Never “accept because price > absorption cost” without justification.
