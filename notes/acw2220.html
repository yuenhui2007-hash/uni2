ACW2220 – Management Accounting 1

Detailed notes: Cost behaviour, CVP analysis, product costing, relevant costing for decisions.

1. Unit Overview

Management accounting provides information to internal managers for planning, control and decision-making. Unlike financial accounting, it is future-oriented, not bound by accounting standards, and emphasises relevance for decisions.

2. Cost Classification

By Behaviour

  • Variable costs – change in total in direct proportion to activity level (e.g. direct materials). Variable cost per unit is constant.
  • Fixed costs – remain constant in total within the relevant range (e.g. factory rent). Fixed cost per unit falls as activity rises.
  • Mixed (semi-variable) costs – contain both fixed and variable components.

By Traceability

  • Direct costs – can be traced conveniently and economically to a cost object.
  • Indirect costs – cannot be traced conveniently; must be allocated.

By Function

  • Manufacturing costs (Direct Materials + Direct Labour + Manufacturing Overhead)
  • Non-manufacturing costs (Selling & Administrative)

3. Cost-Volume-Profit (CVP) Analysis

Contribution Margin (CM) = Sales − All Variable Costs
CM is the amount that contributes first to covering fixed costs and then to profit.
CM per unit = Selling Price − Variable Cost per unit
CM Ratio = CM / Sales

Break-even point (units) = Fixed Costs / CM per unit
Break-even point (RM) = Fixed Costs / CM Ratio

Units for Target Profit = (Fixed Costs + Target Profit) / CM per unit

Assumptions of CVP

  • Selling price, variable cost per unit and total fixed costs are constant
  • Costs are linear
  • Sales mix is constant (multi-product)
  • Inventory levels do not change
CVP Example
SP = RM80, VC = RM50, Fixed costs = RM90,000, Target profit = RM30,000
CM per unit = 30
Break-even units = 90,000 / 30 = 3,000 units
Break-even sales = 3,000 × 80 = RM240,000
Units for target profit = (90,000 + 30,000) / 30 = 4,000 units

4. Relevant Costing for Decisions

Relevant Cost – a future cost that differs between alternatives. Sunk costs are irrelevant. Allocated fixed costs that do not change are usually irrelevant.

Special Order Decision

With spare capacity: accept if the special-order price covers incremental variable costs (and any extra fixed costs) and generates a positive contribution.

Normal SP RM100, VC RM60. Customer offers RM75 for 500 units. Spare capacity exists, no extra fixed costs.
Incremental profit = (75 − 60) × 500 = RM7,500 → Accept

Make or Buy Decision

Compare relevant cost to make (DM + DL + Variable OH + any avoidable fixed costs) with the purchase price. Ignore allocated fixed costs that continue either way.

Make: DM 20 + DL 15 + Var OH 8 + Allocated Fixed 10 = 53
Relevant make cost = 20+15+8 = 43 (fixed continues)
Supplier quote = 40 → Buy (saves 3 per unit)

Keep or Drop a Product Line

Focus on avoidable costs and lost contribution margin. If the contribution margin lost exceeds the fixed costs saved, keep the line.

5. Product Costing Systems (Overview)

  • Job Costing – costs collected by individual job (customised products/services).
  • Process Costing – costs averaged over large numbers of identical units.
  • Activity-Based Costing (ABC) – overhead allocated using multiple activity cost drivers for more accurate product costs.

6. Common Mistakes

  • Including sunk costs or unchanged allocated fixed costs in decisions.
  • Using total costs instead of incremental costs for special orders.
  • Forgetting that fixed cost per unit changes with volume.
  • Mixing contribution margin with gross profit.

7. Exam Tips

  • Always identify what changes between alternatives — only those items are relevant.
  • Prepare a contribution-format income statement for CVP questions.
  • State assumptions when using CVP.
  • Show clear workings with labels (DM, DL, OH, etc.).
  • End every decision question with a clear recommendation and brief reason.
