BFW1001 – Foundations of Finance

Detailed notes: Time value of money, valuation, capital budgeting, financial system, risk & return.

1. Unit Overview

This unit builds the language and basic toolkit of finance. You will master the time value of money, learn how to value simple cash flow streams, evaluate investment projects, and understand the structure of the financial system and the risk-return relationship.

2. Core Concept: Time Value of Money (TVM)

Time Value of Money
A sum of money today is worth more than the same sum in the future because of its potential earning capacity (opportunity cost), inflation, and risk.

Key Variables

  • PV – Present Value (value today)
  • FV – Future Value (value at a future date)
  • r – interest rate / discount rate per period
  • n – number of periods
  • PMT – periodic payment (annuity)

3. Essential Formulas (Memorise These)

Single Sum

Future Value: FV = PV × (1 + r)n
Present Value: PV = FV / (1 + r)n = FV × (1 + r)−n

Ordinary Annuity (payments at end of each period)

FV of Annuity: FVA = PMT × [(1 + r)n − 1] / r
PV of Annuity: PVA = PMT × [1 − (1 + r)−n] / r

Net Present Value (NPV)

NPV = −Initial Investment + Σ [CFt / (1 + r)t]

Decision rule: Accept if NPV > 0; Reject if NPV < 0. When choosing between mutually exclusive projects, pick the highest positive NPV.

4. Worked Examples (Step-by-Step)

Example 1 – Future Value
Invest RM10,000 today at 6% p.a. for 5 years (annual compounding).
FV = 10,000 × (1.06)5 = 10,000 × 1.338226 = RM13,382
Example 2 – Present Value
Need RM20,000 in 4 years. Discount rate 8%.
PV = 20,000 / (1.08)4 = 20,000 / 1.36049 ≈ RM14,701
Example 3 – NPV
Project costs RM50,000. Cash flows: Year 1 RM18,000; Year 2 RM22,000; Year 3 RM25,000. Required return 10%.
PV1 = 18,000 / 1.10 = 16,364
PV2 = 22,000 / 1.21 = 18,182
PV3 = 25,000 / 1.331 ≈ 18,783
Total PV of inflows = 53,329
NPV = 53,329 − 50,000 = +RM3,329 → Accept
Example 4 – PV of Ordinary Annuity
Receive RM4,000 at the end of each year for 5 years. Discount rate 9%.
PVA = 4,000 × [1 − (1.09)−5] / 0.09
(1.09)−5 ≈ 0.6499
[1 − 0.6499] / 0.09 = 3.8897
PVA = 4,000 × 3.8897 ≈ RM15,559

5. Capital Budgeting Basics

  • NPV – preferred method; directly measures value added.
  • IRR – discount rate that makes NPV = 0. Accept if IRR > required return.
  • Payback Period – time to recover initial investment. Simple but ignores TVM and cash flows after payback.

When ranking mutually exclusive projects, NPV is generally superior to IRR (especially with different sizes or timing of cash flows).

6. Financial System Overview

  • Financial markets – bring savers and borrowers together (money market for short-term, capital market for long-term).
  • Financial institutions – banks, insurance companies, unit trusts, pension funds, etc.
  • Financial instruments – equity (shares), debt (bonds, loans), derivatives.
  • Primary market – new issues; Secondary market – trading of existing securities.

7. Risk and Return

Risk–Return Trade-off
Investors require higher expected returns to compensate for bearing higher risk. Low-risk assets (e.g. government securities) offer lower expected returns; high-risk assets must offer higher expected returns to attract capital.

Diversification can reduce unsystematic (company-specific) risk. Systematic risk (market risk) cannot be diversified away and is the risk that is priced in the market.

8. Common Mistakes

  • Using the wrong number of periods (e.g. mixing years and months).
  • Forgetting that NPV already incorporates the required return — do not compare NPV with the discount rate.
  • Confusing ordinary annuity (end of period) with annuity due (beginning of period).
  • Adding cash flows instead of discounting them.

9. Exam Tips

  • Draw a timeline for every multi-period problem.
  • Write the formula first, then substitute numbers.
  • Show intermediate PV factors — markers give method marks.
  • State the decision clearly (“Accept because NPV > 0”).
  • Practise both calculator and manual (formula) methods.
