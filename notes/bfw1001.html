BFW1001 – Foundations of Finance

Advanced notes: valuation algebra, project cash-flow construction, capital budgeting conflicts, risk decomposition

1. Value Maximisation as the Benchmark Objective

Introductory corporate finance adopts shareholder-wealth maximisation (or firm-value maximisation) as the normative objective under complete contracting and well-functioning markets. In the presence of agency conflicts, asymmetric information and externalities, this benchmark is qualified — but exam technique still uses NPV as the operational expression of value creation unless the question explicitly introduces constraints (capital rationing, ESG mandates, stakeholder trade-offs).

Three decision classes: investment (which real assets), financing (claims against the firm), payout/liquidity (distributions and net working capital). Separation of investment and financing (Modigliani–Miller ideal) is taught as a baseline; when capital structure matters, WACC and adjusted present value appear in later units.

2. Discounted Cash Flow Algebra

Present value is the current market value of a promised (or expected) future payoff, obtained by discounting at a rate that compensates for time and risk. Arbitrage-free pricing in frictionless markets equates PV to the cost of a replicating portfolio.
FV = PV(1+r)n
PV = FV(1+r)−n
PVordinary annuity = PMT · an⌉r = PMT[1−(1+r)−n]/r
PVperpetuity = PMT/r  (first cash flow one period ahead)
PVgrowing perpetuity = C₁/(r−g)  (r>g)

Effective annual rate (EAR) vs quoted APR: EAR = (1 + rnom/m)m − 1. Always align compounding frequency with cash-flow frequency. Annuity due = ordinary annuity × (1+r).

3. Project Cash Flows — Construction Rules

NPV uses incremental free cash flows, not accounting profit. Standard adjustments:

  • Add back non-cash charges (depreciation) after tax as taught in your unit sequence
  • Tax on operating earnings; treat tax shields consistently with the discount rate chosen
  • ΔNWC: increases are cash outflows; recover at horizon if operations wind down
  • Opportunity cost of assets already owned (forgone after-tax sale proceeds)
  • Side effects: eroded contribution from existing products (cannibalisation)
  • Ignore sunk costs; ignore interest expense in FCF if using WACC (financing in the rate)

4. Capital Budgeting Criteria — Comparative Statics

NPV = −I₀ + Σt=1..T CFt/(1+r)t

NPV > 0 increases wealth at the risk-adjusted opportunity cost of capital. NPV is additive across independent projects (absent interactions).

IRR: solve NPV(r*) = 0. Accept independent projects if r* > hurdle. Pathologies: non-conventional cash flows → multiple real positive IRRs (Descartes’ rule of signs); mutually exclusive ranking conflicts when scale or timing differs (crossover rate analysis); reinvestment assumption at IRR vs at WACC for NPV.

Profitability index PI = PV(future CF)/I₀ useful under hard capital rationing for single-period constraint ranking (with care for indivisibilities).

Payback / discounted payback: liquidity heuristics; truncated horizon ignores terminal value — inconsistent with value maximisation as a sole criterion.

Ranking conflict sketch: Project A small, high IRR, modest NPV; Project B large, lower IRR, higher NPV. Under unconstrained capital and value maximisation, choose B. IRR alone misleads.

5. Risk, Diversification and Cost of Capital Intuition

Return variance decomposes into systematic and idiosyncratic components. Portfolio diversification drives idiosyncratic risk toward zero; covariance with the market portfolio remains. Equilibrium models (CAPM intuition at this level) price only non-diversifiable risk: E[Ri] = Rf + βi(E[Rm]−Rf).

Using a company WACC on a project with β different from the firm’s asset beta biases accept/reject decisions. Pure-play or subjective adjustment of discount rates is the practical response at introductory level.

6. Financial System Architecture

Direct finance (markets) vs intermediated finance (banks, funds). Primary vs secondary markets; money vs capital markets. Market efficiency hypotheses (weak/semi-strong/strong) constrain the scope for “free” abnormal returns and motivate reliance on DCF with public information — discuss only to the depth of your syllabus.

Exam targets: timeline precision; EAR conversions; full incremental CF list before discounting; explicit comparison of NPV vs IRR failure modes; state the wealth-maximisation link in the conclusion sentence.
