BFW1001 – Foundations of Finance

Degree-level: TVM mechanics, capital budgeting logic, risk pricing intuition, market structure

1. Corporate Finance Decision Map

Finance studies intertemporal allocation of resources under uncertainty. For the firm, the core problems are:

  • Investment (capital budgeting): which real assets to acquire
  • Financing (capital structure): debt vs equity mix and security design
  • Payout and liquidity: dividends/repurchases and working-capital policy

The normative benchmark in introductory corporate finance is maximisation of firm value (shareholder wealth), subject to risk and capital-market constraints. Exam answers should link techniques (NPV, etc.) back to this objective — not treat formulas as ends in themselves.

2. Time Value of Money — Theory and Mechanics

A unit of currency today is worth more than the same unit in the future because of (i) the opportunity cost of capital (forgone return), (ii) inflation (purchasing-power erosion), and (iii) risk (uncertainty of future payoffs).

Compounding maps present value to future value; discounting is the inverse operation. The discount rate is the required return for cash flows of comparable risk and timing. Mismatching the period of the rate and the number of periods is a common exam error (e.g. using an annual rate with monthly cash flows without conversion).

3. Capital Budgeting — Cash-Flow Discipline

Project evaluation uses incremental after-tax cash flows: with-project minus without-project. Include side effects (cannibalisation, synergies), opportunity costs of existing assets, and net working-capital investment (recovered at project end if appropriate). Exclude sunk costs. Do not deduct interest in the cash flows if it is already embedded in the discount rate (avoid double-counting financing costs).

3.1 Net Present Value (primary criterion)

NPV is the expected increase in wealth from accepting the project, measured in present-value terms. Decision rule for independent projects: accept if NPV > 0. For mutually exclusive projects of comparable risk, choose the higher NPV (subject to capital rationing techniques beyond basic NPV when constraints bind).

3.2 IRR and Payback — uses and failure modes

IRR is the rate that sets NPV = 0. Accept if IRR > hurdle rate for independent projects. Problems: multiple IRRs with sign-changing cash flows; ranking conflicts with NPV for mutually exclusive projects of different scale or timing; implicit reinvestment assumption at the IRR (often unrealistic).

Payback measures recovery speed. Ignores time value (unless discounted payback) and cash flows beyond the cutoff. Acceptable as a liquidity or risk screen, not as the sole value-maximising rule.

4. Risk and Return

Risk–return trade-off: rational investors require higher expected returns to hold higher-risk claims. Required return is the opportunity cost of capital for cash flows of that risk class.

Total risk (e.g. return standard deviation) decomposes into:

  • Unsystematic (idiosyncratic) risk — firm-specific; diversifiable in a portfolio
  • Systematic (market) risk — common factors; not diversifiable; priced in equilibrium

For diversified investors, only systematic risk commands a risk premium. Using a firm-wide WACC on a project with different systematic risk misvalues the project (too high NPV for riskier projects if WACC is too low, and vice versa).

5. Financial System

Markets and intermediaries channel funds from surplus to deficit units, provide liquidity, risk-sharing and information/price discovery. Distinguish primary markets (issuance) from secondary markets (trading). Money markets: short-term instruments; capital markets: long-term debt and equity.

Exam technique: Draw timelines; state the formula before substituting; specify the decision rule and numerical conclusion. When comparing NPV and IRR, discuss ranking conflicts and reinvestment assumptions explicitly for higher marks.
