FINANCE • LEVEL 1

BFW1001 – Foundations of Finance

Core concepts of finance, financial mathematics, investment analysis, the financial system and risk.

1. Unit Overview

This unit provides a foundation in finance. You will learn the language of finance, how to value money across time, basic investment decision rules, the structure of the financial system, and the relationship between risk and return.

2. Key Definitions

Time Value of Money (TVM)
A ringgit today is worth more than a ringgit in the future because of its earning potential (interest/opportunity). Money has a time value due to opportunity cost, inflation and risk.
Present Value (PV)
The current worth of a future sum of money or stream of cash flows, discounted at an appropriate rate.
Future Value (FV)
The value of a current sum of money at a future date after it has earned interest/compounded.
Discount Rate / Required Rate of Return
The rate used to discount future cash flows. It reflects the opportunity cost of capital and the risk of the cash flows.
Capital Budgeting
The process of evaluating and selecting long-term investments (projects, assets) that are expected to generate cash flows over more than one year.

3. Core Formulas

Future Value of a Single Sum

FV = PV × (1 + r)n

Present Value of a Single Sum

PV = FV / (1 + r)n    or    PV = FV × (1 + r)-n

Future Value of an Ordinary Annuity

FVA = PMT × [(1 + r)n – 1] / r

Present Value of an Ordinary Annuity

PVA = PMT × [1 – (1 + r)-n] / r

Net Present Value (NPV)

NPV = Σ [CFt / (1 + r)t] – Initial Investment

Accept project if NPV > 0.

Simple Interest vs Compound Interest

Simple: Interest = Principal × Rate × Time
Compound: Interest is earned on interest (power of compounding)

4. Key Concepts

  • Risk–Return Trade-off: Higher expected return is required for higher risk.
  • Diversification: Spreading investments reduces unsystematic risk.
  • Financial System: Markets (money market, capital market), institutions (banks, insurers, funds), and instruments (equity, debt, derivatives).
  • Primary vs Secondary Markets: New issues vs trading of existing securities.
  • Money Market: Short-term, highly liquid, low-risk instruments (T-bills, commercial paper).
  • Capital Market: Longer-term instruments (shares, bonds).

5. Worked Examples

Example 1 – Future Value
You invest RM10,000 today at 6% p.a. compounded annually for 5 years.
FV = 10,000 × (1.06)5 = 10,000 × 1.3382 = RM13,382
Example 2 – Present Value
You will receive RM20,000 in 4 years. Discount rate is 8%.
PV = 20,000 / (1.08)4 = 20,000 / 1.3605 ≈ RM14,701
Example 3 – NPV Decision
Project costs RM50,000. Expected cash flows: Year 1 RM20,000; Year 2 RM25,000; Year 3 RM18,000. Required return 10%.
PV of inflows = 20k/1.1 + 25k/1.21 + 18k/1.331 ≈ 18,182 + 20,661 + 13,523 = 52,366
NPV = 52,366 – 50,000 = +RM2,366 → Accept

6. Exam Tips

  • Memorise the four main TVM formulas and know when to use each.
  • Draw timelines for multi-period problems.
  • NPV is preferred over IRR for ranking projects (especially mutually exclusive).
  • Understand the difference between ordinary annuity and annuity due.
  • Show all workings; calculators are usually allowed but method marks matter.
