Definition bank — finance

Definition

Time value of money

A ringgit today is worth more than a ringgit in the future because it can be invested to earn a return (opportunity cost of waiting).

Example: RM1,000 today at 8% grows to RM1,080 in one year; so RM1,080 in one year is only worth RM1,000 today at 8%.
Definition

Present value (PV)

The current worth of a future cash flow (or stream), discounted at an appropriate rate of return for risk and timing.

Example: PV of RM10,000 in 3 years at 10% = 10,000/1.1³ ≈ RM7,513.
Definition

NPV

Present value of project cash inflows minus present value of cash outflows (including initial investment).

Decision rule: Independent project: accept if NPV > 0 (adds shareholder value after covering cost of capital).
Definition

IRR

The discount rate that sets NPV = 0. The break-even rate of return on the project’s cash flows.

Careful: Mutually exclusive projects — higher IRR can lose to higher NPV (scale / timing).
Definition

Annuity

A series of equal cash flows at regular intervals for a fixed number of periods (ordinary = end of period; due = beginning).

Example: RM2,000 rent paid at the end of each of 12 months is an ordinary annuity (if monthly rate matched).
Definition

Perpetuity

Equal cash flows at regular intervals forever. PV = C / r (no growth); growing: C₁/(r−g) if r>g.

Example: Preferred dividend RM500 forever at 5% required return → PV = 10,000.
Key concept

Opportunity cost of capital

Return investors could earn on a comparable-risk investment elsewhere. Used as the discount rate so NPV measures value created above that baseline.

Example: If shareholders can get 12% on similar-risk assets, a project earning only 9% in PV terms destroys value (negative NPV).

1. What finance is deciding

Corporate finance allocates resources across time under uncertainty. Three decision blocks:

  • Investment — which real assets / projects (capital budgeting)
  • Financing — debt vs equity and contract design
  • Payout & liquidity — dividends, buybacks, working capital

Benchmark objective in this unit: maximise firm value / shareholder wealth. NPV is the operational tool that measures value created at the opportunity cost of capital.

2. Time value of money

A ringgit today is worth more than a ringgit in the future because of (1) opportunity cost of capital, (2) inflation, (3) risk that the future payment may not arrive as expected.
Lump sum
FV = PV × (1+r)n
PV = FV / (1+r)n = FV × (1+r)−n

Always match the period of r with n. If cash flows are monthly, convert annual rate to monthly: rm = rannual/12 (nominal/APR style as taught), or use EAR when the question requires effective rates.

EAR = (1 + rnom/m)m − 1
FV: 10,000 for 5 years at 8% annual → 10,000×(1.08)5 = 10,000×1.469328 = 14,693
PV: need 20,000 in 4 years, r=9% → 20,000/(1.09)4 = 20,000/1.411582 = 14,168
Do notMismatch the period of r and n (e.g. annual rate with monthly periods without converting).
  1. Draw the timeline and mark t = 0.
  2. Match r to the compounding period of n.
  3. Apply FV or PV formula; show the factor.
  4. State the answer with currency and timing.
Self-check — TVM
  1. PV of 15,000 in 4 years at 9%?
  2. What is EAR used for?

3. Annuities and perpetuities

Ordinary annuity: level payments at end of each period.
Annuity due: level payments at beginning of each period → PV and FV are higher by a factor of (1+r) vs ordinary.

PVA = PMT × [1 − (1+r)−n] / r
FVA = PMT × [(1+r)n − 1] / r
PVperpetuity = PMT / r  (first payment one period from now)
PVgrowing perpetuity = C₁ / (r − g)  with r > g
PMT 5,000 end of each year for 6 years, r=7%.
Factor = [1−(1.07)−6]/0.07 ≈ 4.7665
PVA = 5,000 × 4.7665 ≈ 23,833

4. Capital budgeting criteria

Decision ruleIndependent project: accept if NPV > 0. Mutually exclusive: choose the higher NPV (same risk class).
NPV = −I₀ + Σ CFt / (1+r)t

Decision: independent project → accept if NPV > 0. Mutually exclusive → higher NPV (same risk class).

IRR: rate that sets NPV = 0. Accept if IRR > required return (independent projects). Problems: multiple IRRs if cash-flow signs flip more than once; ranking can conflict with NPV when scale/timing differs.

Payback: years to recover I₀. Ignores TVM (unless discounted) and cash flows after cutoff — secondary screen only.

5. Building project cash flows

  • Use incremental after-tax cash flows (with project − without)
  • Include side effects (cannibalisation) and opportunity costs
  • Include ΔNWC (outflow at start; recover at end if applicable)
  • Ignore sunk costs
  • Do not deduct interest in FCF if discounting at WACC (financing is in the rate)
Memory aidIncremental · after-tax · include opportunity cost & ΔNWC · ignore sunk · don’t deduct interest if discounting at WACC.
Self-check — Project cash flows
  1. Should sunk costs enter NPV?
  2. Why might ΔNWC appear as an outflow at t = 0?

6. Risk and return

Investors require higher expected returns for higher risk. Only systematic risk is priced in a diversified portfolio; unsystematic risk can be diversified away.

If you discount a high-β project at a firm-wide WACC that is too low, NPV is overstated → may accept value-destroying projects.

7. Worked capital budgeting set

I₀ = 100,000. CF₁=40,000; CF₂=45,000; CF₃=50,000. r = 12%.
PV₁ = 40,000/1.12 = 35,714
PV₂ = 45,000/1.2544 = 35,873
PV₃ = 50,000/1.404928 = 35,589
Σ PV = 107,176
NPV = 107,176 − 100,000 = +7,176 → ACCEPT

Interpretation: project adds about RM7,176 of value at a 12% opportunity cost of capital.
Compare two projects (same risk): A NPV=5,000 IRR=28%; B NPV=18,000 IRR=16%. Unconstrained value maximisation → choose B (higher NPV). IRR alone would wrongly prefer A.

10. Financial system, institutions and regulation

Surplus units supply funds; deficit units demand funds. Channels: direct finance (markets) and indirect/intermediated finance (banks, funds). Money markets = short-term, capital markets = long-term. Primary market = issuance; secondary market = trading (liquidity for investors).

Regulation aims at systemic stability, investor protection and market integrity (disclosure, licensing, conduct rules). Know the role of regulation in your exam answers, not only lists of agency names.

11. Debt securities (exam awareness)

Bonds/notes promise contractual cash flows (coupons + principal). Price is the PV of remaining cash flows discounted at the required yield. Inverse relationship: yields ↑ → bond prices ↓. Par / premium / discount relative to coupon vs market yield.

Bond price ≈ Σ Coupont/(1+y)t + Face/(1+y)n
Face 1,000; annual coupon 60 (6%); 3 years; y=8%.
Price = 60/1.08 + 60/1.08² + 1,060/1.08³ ≈ 55.56 + 51.44 + 840.98 = 947.98 (discount bond)

12. Equity securities

Ordinary shares: residual claim, dividends not contractual. Valuation intuition: PV of expected future dividends (Gordon growth model if constant growth taught): P₀ = D₁/(r−g), r>g. Preference shares: hybrid features (fixed dividend priority).

13. Foreign exchange (intro)

Exchange rate = price of one currency in terms of another. Appreciation/depreciation affects importers/exporters. Spot vs forward rates (if in syllabus). Business exposure: transaction risk on foreign-currency payables/receivables.

15. Mixed TVM problem bank (worked)

A. Ordinary annuity PV
PMT 4,000 end of year for 8 years, r = 6%.
Factor = [1 − (1.06)−8]/0.06 ≈ 6.2098
PV = 4,000 × 6.2098 = 24,839
B. Annuity due (payments at beginning)
PVdue = PVordinary × (1+r)
If ordinary PV = 24,839 at 6%, due PV = 24,839 × 1.06 = 26,329
C. Growing perpetuity
Next cash flow C₁ = 12,000; r = 10%; g = 3%.
PV = 12,000/(0.10−0.03) = 171,429
D. Two-stage
CF 5,000 for years 1–3, then 20,000 at end of year 3 only once more? (treat carefully)
Better: PV of 3-year annuity 5,000 at 8% + PV of lump sums as specified.
PVA = 5,000 × [1−1.08−3]/0.08 ≈ 5,000 × 2.5771 = 12,885

16. NPV vs IRR conflict — numerical

Project S (small): I₀=10,000; CF₁=12,500. r=10%.
NPVS = 12,500/1.1 − 10,000 = 1,364
IRRS ≈ 25%

Project L (large): I₀=50,000; CF₁=60,000. r=10%.
NPVL = 60,000/1.1 − 50,000 = 4,545
IRRL ≈ 20%

IRR prefers S; NPV prefers L. Wealth maximisation → choose L.

17. Bond price sensitivity

Face 1,000; coupon 70 annual; n=5; yield 7% → price = par 1,000
If yield rises to 9%:
Price = 70×[1−1.09−5]/0.09 + 1,000/1.095 ≈ 70×3.8897 + 649.93 ≈ 922 (discount)
If yield falls to 5%:
Price ≈ 70×4.3295 + 783.53 ≈ 1,087 (premium)
Memory aidYield ↑ → price ↓. Coupon < yield → discount; coupon > yield → premium.

18. Risk classes and discount rates

  • Risk-free rate approximates time value with no default risk
  • Risk premium rises with systematic risk (β) in CAPM logic
  • Using a firm-wide WACC on a riskier project overstates NPV
  • Using too high a rate on a safe project rejects value-creating investments
Do notTreat unsystematic risk as requiring a higher discount rate if investors hold diversified portfolios — only systematic risk is priced under standard models taught at intro level.
