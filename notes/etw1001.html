Definition bank — statistics

Definition

Population vs sample

Population: the entire set of interest. Sample: a subset used to infer about the population.

Example: All Monash Malaysia students = population; 200 randomly surveyed students = sample.
Definition

Parameter vs statistic

Parameter: numerical feature of a population (e.g. μ, σ). Statistic: numerical feature of a sample (e.g. x̄, s).

Example: True mean CGPA of all students (μ) vs mean CGPA in your sample (x̄).
Definition

Variance & standard deviation

Measure of spread around the mean. Variance is average squared deviation; SD is its square root (same units as data). Sample variance divides by n−1.

Example: Data 2,4,6 → mean 4; sample s² = [(−2)²+0+2²]/2 = 4; s = 2.
Definition

Confidence interval

A range constructed from sample data such that, under repeated sampling, a stated percentage (e.g. 95%) of such intervals would contain the true parameter.

Form: estimate ± critical value × standard error.
Definition

Null hypothesis (H₀)

The baseline claim under test, usually of “no effect / no difference / parameter equals a value”. We reject or fail to reject it; we do not “prove” it true.

Example: H₀: μ = 30 minutes mean delivery time.
Definition

p-value

Probability, assuming H₀ is true, of obtaining a result at least as extreme as the one observed. Small p-value → data are surprising under H₀ → evidence against H₀.

Example: p = 0.01 at α = 0.05 → reject H₀.
Key concept

Slope in simple regression

In Ŷ = a + bX, b is the estimated change in Y associated with a one-unit increase in X, on average, in the fitted linear model.

Example: Ŷ = 40 + 2.5(hours); each extra study hour associates with +2.5 marks predicted, holding the linear model.

1. Descriptive statistics

Interpretation marksNumbers alone are mid-band. Say what the statistic means in business language.

Mean x̄ = Σx/n — sensitive to outliers.
Median — middle value; robust to outliers; prefer when skewed.
Mode — most frequent.

Sample variance s² = Σ(x − x̄)² / (n − 1)
Sample SD s = √s²

Use n−1 for sample variance (unbiased estimator of σ² under standard assumptions). Range = max−min; IQR = Q3−Q1.

Data: 5,7,9,11,13. Mean=9.
Deviations²: 16+4+0+4+16=40 → s²=40/4=10 → s=√10≈3.16
Memory aidSample variance uses n − 1. Mean is pulled by outliers; median is robust — prefer median when skewed.

3. Inference

Parameter = population (μ, σ, p). Statistic = sample (x̄, s, p̂).

CI idea: x̄ ± critical value × SE. 95% CI: in repeated sampling, ~95% of such intervals capture the true mean. Do not say “P(μ in this interval)=0.95” after seeing the data (frequentist).

Hypothesis test steps:

  1. H₀ and H₁
  2. α (often 0.05)
  3. Test statistic
  4. p-value or critical value
  5. Reject or fail to reject H₀
  6. Business conclusion in words

Type I: reject true H₀. Type II: fail to reject false H₀. Power = 1 − β.

  1. State H₀ and H₁ in symbols and words.
  2. Choose α (often 0.05) and the test.
  3. Compute test statistic / p-value.
  4. Reject or fail to reject H₀ — never “accept H₀”.
  5. Write a business conclusion in plain language.
Do notSay “prove” from a significance test. You only have evidence against H₀ at level α.

4. Simple linear regression

Ŷ = b₀ + b₁X

b₁ = estimated average change in Y for +1 unit X.
b₀ = predicted Y when X=0 (may be meaningless if X=0 is outside data range).
R² = fraction of sample variation in Y explained by X — not proof of causality.

Ŷ = 15 + 3.2X (X = advertising RM’000, Y = sales RM’000)
Slope: each extra RM1,000 advertising associated with ~RM3,200 higher sales on average.
Do notClaim “X causes Y” from observational regression alone. Report association; discuss causality separately.

8. Confidence interval template

  1. Point estimate x̄
  2. SE = s/√n (or σ/√n if σ known)
  3. Critical value (z or t as taught)
  4. CI = x̄ ± critical × SE
  5. Interpret: long-run capture rate language — not “probability μ is in this interval” after seeing data (frequentist)
x̄=50, s=10, n=25, approx z=1.96 for 95%
SE=2; CI ≈ 50 ± 3.92 → [46.08, 53.92]

9. Hypothesis test write-up template

H₀: μ = μ₀
H₁: μ ≠ μ₀ (or one-sided)
Test stat ≈ (x̄ − μ₀)/(s/√n)
Claim μ=100; n=36; x̄=104; s=12; α=0.05 two-sided
SE=2; z=(104−100)/2=2
|2|>1.96 → reject H₀
Business: evidence mean differs from 100 (appears higher)

11. Choosing the right measure (exam judgment)

Use the mean when
  • Data are roughly symmetric
  • No extreme outliers
  • You need a measure that uses all values
Use the median when
  • Distribution is skewed
  • Outliers are present
  • You want a “typical” robust centre
Salaries (RM’000): 3.5, 3.8, 4.0, 4.2, 18.0
Mean = 6.7 (misleading — pulled by 18)
Median = 4.0 (better “typical” salary)

12. Two-sample intuition (if taught)

Compare means of two groups (e.g. campaign A vs B). Same logic: hypotheses, SE of difference, test statistic, p-value, business conclusion. Always state whether samples are independent and whether variances are assumed equal if your unit uses that version.
