ETW1001 – Introduction to Statistical Analysis

Degree-level: descriptive foundations, inference logic, regression interpretation, decision errors

1. From Data to Decision

Statistics supports business decisions under uncertainty. Distinguish descriptive analytics (summarise observed data) from inferential analytics (generalise from sample to population with quantified uncertainty). Degree assessments test correct choice of measure, valid interpretation, and awareness of assumptions — not only computation.

2. Descriptive Statistics

Central tendency: mean (sensitive to outliers), median (robust), mode. Use median for skewed distributions or ordinal data where mean is misleading.

Sample variance s² = Σ(x − x̄)² / (n − 1)
Sample standard deviation s = √s²

Division by n−1 makes s² an unbiased estimator of population variance under standard sampling assumptions. Range and IQR summarise spread; z-scores standardise relative position.

3. Sampling and Inference Logic

A parameter describes a population; a statistic is computed from a sample. Sampling distributions describe how a statistic varies across samples; the standard error shrinks as n increases (for many estimators), improving precision.

Confidence interval: a range constructed so that, under repeated sampling, a stated proportion (e.g. 95%) of such intervals contain the true parameter. It is incorrect to say “there is 95% probability the parameter lies in this specific interval” in the frequentist interpretation usually taught at this level — state the repeated-sampling meaning carefully for full marks.

4. Hypothesis Testing

  1. State H₀ and H₁ (one-sided vs two-sided matters)
  2. Choose α (Type I error rate)
  3. Compute test statistic and p-value (or critical value)
  4. Reject H₀ if p ≤ α
  5. Interpret in business language — statistical significance ≠ practical significance

Type I error: reject true H₀. Type II error: fail to reject false H₀. Power = 1 − β. Trade-off between α and β for fixed n.

5. Simple Linear Regression

Ŷ = b₀ + b₁X

b₁: estimated average change in Y for a one-unit increase in X. b₀: predicted Y when X = 0 (may lack practical meaning if X = 0 is outside the data range). R²: proportion of sample variation in Y explained by the model — high R² does not prove causality. Residual analysis and awareness of nonlinearity, outliers and omitted variables are part of competent interpretation.

Exam technique: Always interpret numbers in context. State hypotheses and conclusions in words. Prefer median when skewness/outliers are indicated. Do not over-claim causality from regression alone.
