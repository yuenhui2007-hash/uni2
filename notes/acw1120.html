ACW1120 – Financial Accounting 1

Deep conceptual notes for exam revision

1. Why Financial Accounting Exists

Businesses need a systematic way to record, classify and communicate economic events. External users (investors, lenders, creditors, regulators) cannot observe the entity’s operations directly, so they rely on general-purpose financial statements. The Conceptual Framework and accounting standards exist to make that information useful, comparable and credible.

Financial accounting is therefore not just “bookkeeping”. It involves judgement about recognition, measurement and disclosure so that the statements faithfully represent the entity’s economic reality.

2. The Conceptual Framework (Core Theory)

Objective of general-purpose financial reporting
To provide financial information about the reporting entity that is useful to existing and potential investors, lenders and other creditors in making decisions relating to providing resources to the entity. Those decisions involve buying, selling or holding equity and debt instruments, and providing or settling loans and other forms of credit.

Users need information about:

  • Economic resources (assets) and claims (liabilities and equity)
  • Changes in those resources and claims (performance and other events)
  • How efficiently and effectively management has discharged its responsibilities (stewardship)

2.1 Qualitative Characteristics – In Depth

Fundamental characteristics (must be present for information to be useful):

  • Relevance – Information is relevant if it is capable of making a difference in decisions. It has predictive value (helps users forecast future outcomes), confirmatory value (helps confirm or correct prior evaluations), or both. Materiality is an entity-specific aspect of relevance: information is material if omitting, misstating or obscuring it could reasonably influence decisions.
  • Faithful representation – Information represents the substance of economic phenomena. It should be as complete, neutral and free from error as possible. Neutrality means without bias in selection or presentation. Faithful representation does not mean perfect accuracy; estimates are often necessary.

Enhancing characteristics (improve usefulness when fundamental characteristics are present):

  • Comparability – Users can identify similarities and differences. Consistency (same methods over time) supports comparability but is not identical to it.
  • Verifiability – Different knowledgeable independent observers could reach consensus that a depiction is faithfully represented (not necessarily a single point estimate).
  • Timeliness – Information is available in time to influence decisions. Older information is less useful.
  • Understandability – Classifying, characterising and presenting information clearly. Complex phenomena should not be excluded merely because they are hard to understand.

There is often a trade-off (e.g. relevance vs perfect accuracy). The Framework requires judgement to maximise overall usefulness.

2.2 Elements – Deep Definitions

Asset – A present economic resource controlled by the entity as a result of past events. An economic resource is a right that has the potential to produce economic benefits. Control means the entity can direct the use of the resource and obtain the benefits.
Liability – A present obligation of the entity to transfer an economic resource as a result of past events. An obligation is a duty or responsibility that the entity has no practical ability to avoid.
Equity – The residual interest in the assets of the entity after deducting all its liabilities. Equity claims are claims against the entity that do not meet the definition of a liability.
Income – Increases in assets, or decreases in liabilities, that result in increases in equity, other than those relating to contributions from equity holders.
Expenses – Decreases in assets, or increases in liabilities, that result in decreases in equity, other than those relating to distributions to equity holders.

Recognition occurs when an item meets the definition of an element and recognition provides useful information (relevant and faithfully representative). Probability of future benefits/outflows and reliable measurement are practical considerations that support this decision.

3. Accrual Basis vs Cash Basis

Under the accrual basis, effects of transactions are recognised when they occur (not only when cash is received or paid). Revenues are recognised when earned; expenses when incurred. This matches effort with accomplishment and better reflects performance and position.

Under the cash basis, only cash receipts and payments are recorded. This can distort performance (e.g. large cash receipt for future services looks like profit). The Conceptual Framework therefore prefers accrual accounting for general-purpose financial statements.

4. The Accounting Equation & Double-Entry Logic

Assets = Liabilities + Equity

Every economic event that is recorded must keep this equation in balance. That is why every transaction has equal debits and credits. Equity expands as:

Equity = Capital + Revenues − Expenses − Drawings

Debit / Credit rules (conceptual):

  • Left side of the equation (assets) increases with debits
  • Right side (liabilities and equity) increases with credits
  • Expenses reduce equity → debit to increase
  • Revenues increase equity → credit to increase

5. The Accounting Cycle – Conceptual Flow

  1. Identify transactions from source documents (invoices, receipts, contracts)
  2. Analyse effect on the accounting equation and decide accounts to debit/credit
  3. Journalise in chronological order (general journal)
  4. Post to ledger accounts (T-accounts / general ledger)
  5. Unadjusted trial balance – list of balances to check debits = credits
  6. Adjusting entries – update accounts for accruals, deferrals, depreciation
  7. Adjusted trial balance
  8. Financial statements
  9. Closing entries – zero temporary accounts (revenues, expenses, drawings) into equity
  10. Post-closing trial balance – only permanent accounts remain

6. Adjusting Entries – Why They Matter

The trial balance after day-to-day recording is incomplete under accrual accounting. Some revenues have been earned but not yet recorded; some expenses have been incurred but not yet recorded; some cash received or paid in advance still sits in the wrong period. Adjusting entries allocate these items to the correct period. They never involve the Cash account.

Depreciation Concept

Depreciation is an allocation process, not a valuation process. It systematically assigns the depreciable amount (cost − residual value) over the asset’s useful life to match the cost of using the asset with the periods that benefit. Accumulated Depreciation is a contra-asset; carrying amount = Cost − Accumulated Depreciation.

Straight-line: Annual depreciation = (Cost − Residual value) / Useful life in years
Cost RM50,000; residual RM5,000; life 9 years → Depreciable amount RM45,000 → Annual dep. RM5,000
Year-end: Dr Depreciation Expense 5,000   Cr Accumulated Depreciation – Equipment 5,000
Carrying amount at end of Year 1 = 50,000 − 5,000 = RM45,000

7. Financial Statements – Purpose and Structure

  • Statement of Profit or Loss (Income Statement) – Performance for a period: Revenues − Expenses = Profit or Loss
  • Statement of Changes in Equity – How equity moved: opening equity + profit − drawings/dividends ± other capital movements
  • Statement of Financial Position (Balance Sheet) – Position at a point in time: Assets = Liabilities + Equity; classified into current and non-current

These statements are linked: profit flows into equity; equity appears on the statement of financial position.

8. Common Exam Traps

  • Using cash basis thinking when the question requires accrual
  • Including Cash in adjusting entries
  • Crediting “Depreciation” instead of Accumulated Depreciation
  • Calculating depreciation on cost instead of (cost − residual)
  • Vague Conceptual Framework answers — always start with the precise definition
Exam strategy: For theory questions, quote or closely paraphrase Framework definitions, then explain. For practical questions, show equation analysis or T-accounts if helpful, and label every journal entry with a short narration.
