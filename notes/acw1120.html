ACW1120 – Financial Accounting 1

Advanced revision notes for closed-book exams: Framework application, recognition judgements, measurement bases, full-cycle mechanics

1. Epistemic Role of Financial Reporting

Financial reporting reduces information asymmetry between insiders (management) and outside capital providers. Agency theory frames managers as agents who may not maximise principal wealth; accounting numbers enter debt covenants, compensation contracts and equity valuation models. Hence recognition and measurement choices are not neutral bookkeeping — they reallocate reported wealth across periods and stakeholders.

General-purpose financial statements target primary users: existing and potential investors, lenders and other creditors. The IASB Conceptual Framework subordinates the information needs of other users (regulators, employees, public) when those conflict with primary-user decision usefulness. Stewardship (accountability for resources) is embedded in the objective but does not independently override decision-usefulness in the current Framework.

2. Conceptual Framework — Operative Tests

Objective: provide financial information about the reporting entity useful to primary users in decisions about providing resources to the entity (buy/sell/hold equity and debt; provide/settle credit), including assessment of management’s stewardship of economic resources.

2.1 Qualitative characteristics as constraints on recognition and measurement

Relevance = capacity to affect decisions through predictive value, confirmatory value, or both. Materiality is an entity-specific screen within relevance: information is material if omitting, misstating or obscuring it could reasonably influence primary users. Materiality is quantitative and qualitative (e.g. related-party fraud may be material despite small amounts).

Faithful representation = depiction of substance of economic phenomena that is complete, neutral and free from error to the maximum extent practicable. It is not a claim of perfect accuracy. Neutrality prohibits biased selection/presentation; the Framework’s discussion of prudence is limited — prudence must not justify systematic understatement of assets/income.

Enhancing characteristics (comparability including consistency, verifiability, timeliness, understandability) cannot compensate for absence of relevance or faithful representation. Exam questions that ask “which characteristic is more important” expect reasoned trade-off analysis (e.g. timely soft estimate vs delayed hard measurement), not a single rigid ranking.

2.2 Definitions of elements (2018 Framework language)

Asset: a present economic resource controlled by the entity as a result of past events. Economic resource = a right that has the potential to produce economic benefits.

Liability: a present obligation of the entity to transfer an economic resource as a result of past events. Obligation = duty/responsibility with no practical ability to avoid.

Equity: residual interest in assets after deducting liabilities.

Income / Expenses: increases / decreases in assets or decreases / increases in liabilities that increase / decrease equity, excluding contributions from / distributions to equity holders.

Control is the ability to direct the use of the economic resource and obtain the benefits (and prevent others from doing so). “Past event” prevents recognition of purely prospective intentions. Potential to produce benefits does not require probability > 50% for the definition; probability and measurement uncertainty enter the recognition decision.

2.3 Recognition and derecognition

Recognition is warranted only when the item meets the definition of an element and recognition provides useful information (relevant + faithfully representable). Low probability of inflow/outflow or very high measurement uncertainty may support non-recognition with disclosure. Derecognition occurs when the entity loses control of an asset or no longer has a present obligation for a liability — exam scenarios often test premature revenue recognition or failure to derecognise transferred assets when risks/rewards (or control) are retained.

2.4 Measurement bases (awareness at this level)

Historical cost, current value (fair value, value in use, fulfilment value), and amortised cost appear across standards. Choice of basis affects volatility of profit, covenant headroom and comparability. Historical cost is verifiable but may lack relevance when prices change; fair value may be relevant but hierarchical Level 3 inputs reduce verifiability.

3. Accrual Basis, Periodicity and Matching

Accrual accounting records economic events when they occur. The periodicity assumption forces allocation of continuous activity into discrete reporting periods — creating the need for adjusting entries. “Matching” is not an independent Framework principle that overrides asset/liability definitions; expenses are recognised when a decrease in assets or increase in liabilities arises that reduces equity (other than distributions). In practice, matching language still appears in textbooks for depreciation and cost of sales.

Cash-basis profit confounds financing, investing and operating timing. Lenders and equity analysts therefore adjust toward accrual measures and cash-flow statements; understanding why accruals dominate GPFS is an exam expectation, not optional colour.

4. The Accounting Equation as a Linear Constraint

A ≡ L + E   with   E = Capital + ΣIncome − ΣExpenses − Drawings

Double-entry is the bookkeeping expression of this identity. Analysing a transaction means specifying the simultaneous change in two or more accounts such that the identity holds. Contra-asset accounts (allowance for doubtful debts, accumulated depreciation) carry credit balances but are presented with assets; they are not liabilities.

5. Cycle, Internal Control and Limitations of the Trial Balance

Flow: source documents → journals (including compound and adjusting entries) → ledger → unadjusted TB → adjustments → adjusted TB → statements → closing → post-closing TB. Subsidiary ledgers (AR, AP) and control accounts reconcile detail to general ledger.

A trial balance only tests equality of debit and credit totals. It does not detect: complete omission of a transaction; entry of equal wrong amounts; errors of principle (capital vs revenue expenditure); compensating errors; wrong account with correct side and amount within the same element class sometimes still balances. Exam questions exploit this limitation.

6. Adjusting Entries — Intra-Period Allocation

Adjustments implement accrual accounting at period-end. Categories:

  • Deferrals (prepayments): cash precedes recognition — prepaid expenses, unearned revenue
  • Accruals: recognition precedes cash — accrued expenses, accrued revenues
  • Estimates: depreciation, impairment allowances (where taught)

Cash never appears in a pure adjusting entry. Reversing entries (optional technique) may be used at the start of the next period for accruals to simplify subsequent cash recording — know the logic if your unit guide covers it.

Depreciation as systematic allocation

Depreciable amount = Cost − Residual value
Straight-line charge per period = Depreciable amount / Useful life

Cost includes purchase price and directly attributable costs to bring the asset to location and condition for intended use (capitalisation rules may be refined in later units under PPE standards). Residual value and useful life are estimates; changes in estimate are applied prospectively. Carrying amount = Cost − Accumulated depreciation (− impairment if applicable). Depreciation continues while the asset is available for use, including periods of idle capacity (unless classified as held for sale under later standards).

Worked multi-year: Cost 120,000; residual 12,000; life 9 years; purchased 1 Apr; year-end 31 Dec; straight-line; full months.
Annual = (120,000−12,000)/9 = 12,000 → monthly = 1,000.
Year 1 (9 months): expense 9,000; carrying amount 31 Dec Y1 = 120,000−9,000 = 111,000.
Year 2: expense 12,000; carrying amount = 99,000.

7. Statement Articulation and Classification

  • P&L: income and expenses for the period → profit or loss
  • SCE: movement in equity components (capital, retained earnings, drawings)
  • SFP: assets = liabilities + equity at reporting date; current vs non-current classification based on operating cycle / 12-month criteria as taught

Closing entries zero income and expense accounts into retained earnings (or capital for sole props) and close drawings. Permanent accounts remain; temporary accounts restart.

8. Exam Discriminators

  • Apply Framework definitions to edge cases (executory contracts, contingent items — recognition vs disclosure)
  • Separate errors of principle from clerical errors
  • Compute partial-period depreciation correctly
  • Prepare a full set of statements from an adjusted TB with additional information
  • Explain why an item fails recognition (definition vs measurement uncertainty vs lack of useful information)
Mark scheme mindset: definitions → criteria → application to facts → conclusion. Unsupported conclusions score poorly. Show all allocation workings.
