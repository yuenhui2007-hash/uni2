ACW1120 – Financial Accounting 1

Introduction to financial accounting guided by the Conceptual Framework and Accounting Standards.

1. Unit Overview

This unit introduces the fundamentals of financial accounting. Emphasis is placed on accounting processes, practices and policies that enable the preparation of general-purpose financial statements. You will integrate theoretical knowledge with technical skills and begin exercising professional judgement.

2. Key Definitions

Financial Accounting
The process of identifying, measuring, recording and communicating financial information about an entity to external users (investors, creditors, regulators) so they can make informed decisions.
Conceptual Framework
A set of fundamental concepts and principles that guide the development of accounting standards and the preparation of financial statements. It defines the objective of financial reporting, qualitative characteristics, elements of financial statements, and recognition/measurement criteria.
Double-entry Accounting
Every transaction affects at least two accounts: for every debit there is an equal and corresponding credit. The accounting equation (Assets = Liabilities + Equity) always remains in balance.
Accrual Basis
Revenues are recognised when earned and expenses when incurred, regardless of when cash is received or paid. This is the basis required by the Conceptual Framework for general-purpose financial statements.
Elements of Financial Statements
  • Assets – present economic resource controlled by the entity as a result of past events.
  • Liabilities – present obligation to transfer an economic resource as a result of past events.
  • Equity – residual interest in the assets after deducting liabilities.
  • Income – increases in assets or decreases in liabilities that result in increases in equity (other than contributions from owners).
  • Expenses – decreases in assets or increases in liabilities that result in decreases in equity (other than distributions to owners).

3. Core Concepts & Processes

The Accounting Equation

Assets = Liabilities + Equity

Expanded form:

Assets = Liabilities + (Owner’s Capital + Revenues – Expenses – Drawings)

The Accounting Cycle (simplified)

  1. Identify and analyse transactions
  2. Record in journal (journal entries)
  3. Post to ledger accounts
  4. Prepare unadjusted trial balance
  5. Record adjusting entries
  6. Prepare adjusted trial balance
  7. Prepare financial statements
  8. Record closing entries
  9. Prepare post-closing trial balance

Adjusting Entries (key types)

  • Prepaid expenses – expense that has been paid in advance (asset → expense)
  • Unearned revenue – cash received before service is performed (liability → revenue)
  • Accrued expenses – expenses incurred but not yet paid
  • Accrued revenues – revenues earned but not yet received
  • Depreciation – systematic allocation of the cost of a non-current asset over its useful life

Qualitative Characteristics (Conceptual Framework)

Fundamental: Relevance and Faithful Representation

Enhancing: Comparability, Verifiability, Timeliness, Understandability

4. Worked Examples

Example 1 – Journal Entry
On 1 March, the business purchased inventory for RM8,000 on credit.

Dr Inventory     8,000
    Cr Accounts Payable     8,000
(To record purchase of inventory on credit)
Example 2 – Adjusting Entry (Prepaid Rent)
On 1 Jan paid RM12,000 for 12 months’ rent. At 31 March (year-end), 3 months have been used.

Dr Rent Expense     3,000
    Cr Prepaid Rent     3,000
(To record rent expense for 3 months)
Example 3 – Simple Income Statement
Revenue RM50,000
Cost of sales RM28,000
Operating expenses RM12,000

Gross profit = 50,000 – 28,000 = RM22,000
Net profit = 22,000 – 12,000 = RM10,000

5. Exam Tips

  • Always start with the Conceptual Framework definitions when answering “explain” questions.
  • Practise journal entries until they become automatic – debit/credit rules are fundamental.
  • Know the difference between cash basis and accrual basis cold.
  • Memorise the qualitative characteristics and the five elements.
  • Show workings clearly; partial marks are common.

6. Common Exam-Style Questions

  1. Define an asset according to the Conceptual Framework and give two examples.
  2. Explain the difference between accrual accounting and cash accounting with an illustration.
  3. Prepare the adjusting entry for depreciation of equipment costing RM40,000 with residual value RM4,000 and useful life 6 years (straight-line).
  4. What are the two fundamental qualitative characteristics of useful financial information?
