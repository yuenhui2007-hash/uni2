ACW1120 – Financial Accounting 1

Conceptual Framework · Double-entry · Adjusting entries · Financial statements

1. Unit Focus

Learn the language of accounting guided by the Conceptual Framework. From transactions to financial statements, with professional judgement.

2. Conceptual Framework Snapshot

Objective of general-purpose financial reporting
Provide financial information useful to investors, lenders and other creditors in making resource-allocation decisions.

Qualitative Characteristics

Elements of Financial Statements

Element Definition (short) Statement
AssetPresent economic resource controlled from past eventsSFP
LiabilityPresent obligation to transfer economic resourceSFP
EquityResidual interest (Assets − Liabilities)SFP / SCE
IncomeIncreases in equity (not from owners)P&L
ExpensesDecreases in equity (not to owners)P&L

3. Accounting Equation

Assets = Liabilities + Equity

Expanded form:

Assets = Liabilities + Capital + Revenues − Expenses − Drawings

4. Accounting Cycle

Journal → Post to Ledger → Unadjusted TB → Adjusting entries → Adjusted TB → Financial Statements → Closing

5. Adjusting Entries (Exam Hotspot)

Straight-line Depreciation

Annual Depreciation = (Cost − Residual Value) ÷ Useful Life
Example: Equipment cost RM48,000, residual RM6,000, life 7 years.
Depreciable amount = 42,000 → Annual dep. = RM6,000

7. Common Mistakes

  • Putting Cash in adjusting entries
  • Crediting “Depreciation” instead of Accumulated Depreciation
  • Using cost (not cost − residual) for straight-line dep.
  • Mixing expense and prepaid accounts
Exam tip: Always write the definition first for Conceptual Framework questions. Show full workings for depreciation and adjustments — method marks count.
