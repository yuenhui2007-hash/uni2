ACW1120 – Financial Accounting 1

Detailed exam-ready notes: Conceptual Framework, double-entry system, adjusting entries, financial statements.

1. Unit Overview & Learning Focus

This unit introduces financial accounting guided by the Conceptual Framework and Accounting Standards. You learn the accounting process from transactions to financial statements, and begin to exercise professional judgement. Emphasis is on understanding why we account the way we do, not just the mechanics.

Key skills tested in exams: journal entries, adjusting entries, preparation of basic financial statements, explanation of Conceptual Framework concepts, and simple analysis.

2. The Conceptual Framework (Must-Know)

Objective of General Purpose Financial Reporting
To provide financial information about the reporting entity that is useful to existing and potential investors, lenders and other creditors in making decisions about providing resources to the entity.

Qualitative Characteristics

Fundamental:

  • Relevance – information can make a difference in decisions (predictive value and/or confirmatory value). Materiality is an aspect of relevance.
  • Faithful Representation – information is complete, neutral and free from error (as far as possible).

Enhancing:

  • Comparability (including consistency)
  • Verifiability
  • Timeliness
  • Understandability

Elements of Financial Statements

Asset – A present economic resource controlled by the entity as a result of past events. An economic resource is a right that has the potential to produce economic benefits.
Liability – A present obligation of the entity to transfer an economic resource as a result of past events.
Equity – The residual interest in the assets of the entity after deducting all its liabilities.
Income – Increases in assets or decreases in liabilities that result in increases in equity, other than contributions from equity holders.
Expenses – Decreases in assets or increases in liabilities that result in decreases in equity, other than distributions to equity holders.

Recognition Criteria (simplified)

An item is recognised when it meets the definition of an element and recognition provides useful information (i.e. it is relevant and can be faithfully represented). In practice this usually requires probability of inflow/outflow of economic benefits and reliable measurement.

3. The Accounting Equation & Double-Entry System

Assets = Liabilities + Equity

Expanded form often used in exams:

Assets = Liabilities + Capital + Revenues − Expenses − Drawings

Debit and Credit Rules (Golden Rules)

  • Assets & Expenses → Debit to increase, Credit to decrease
  • Liabilities, Equity & Revenues → Credit to increase, Debit to decrease

Every transaction has at least one debit and one credit of equal amount. The equation always stays in balance.

4. The Accounting Cycle (Step-by-Step)

  1. Identify and analyse transactions from source documents
  2. Record journal entries (general journal)
  3. Post to ledger accounts (T-accounts)
  4. Prepare unadjusted trial balance
  5. Record adjusting entries
  6. Prepare adjusted trial balance
  7. Prepare financial statements (Income Statement, Statement of Changes in Equity, Statement of Financial Position)
  8. Record closing entries
  9. Prepare post-closing trial balance

5. Adjusting Entries (High-Frequency Exam Topic)

Adjusting entries are made at the end of the period to update accounts under the accrual basis. They never involve cash.

Type 1 – Prepaid Expenses (Asset → Expense)

Example: Paid RM12,000 rent on 1 Jan for 12 months. Year-end 31 March → 3 months expired.

Dr Rent Expense 3,000
   Cr Prepaid Rent 3,000

Type 2 – Unearned Revenue (Liability → Revenue)

Example: Received RM6,000 on 1 Nov for 6 months’ service. Year-end 31 Dec → 2 months earned.

Dr Unearned Revenue 2,000
   Cr Service Revenue 2,000

Type 3 – Accrued Expenses (Expense + Liability)

Example: Salaries earned by employees but not yet paid RM4,500.

Dr Salaries Expense 4,500
   Cr Salaries Payable 4,500

Type 4 – Accrued Revenues (Asset + Revenue)

Example: Services performed but not yet billed RM3,200.

Dr Accounts Receivable 3,200
   Cr Service Revenue 3,200

Type 5 – Depreciation

Straight-line formula:

Annual Depreciation = (Cost − Residual Value) / Useful Life
Dr Depreciation Expense xxx
   Cr Accumulated Depreciation – Asset xxx

Accumulated Depreciation is a contra-asset account. Carrying amount = Cost − Accumulated Depreciation.

6. Financial Statements (Basic Structure)

Income Statement (Statement of Profit or Loss)

Revenues − Expenses = Profit (or Loss) for the period.

Statement of Changes in Equity

Opening Capital + Profit − Drawings + Additional Capital = Closing Capital (Equity).

Statement of Financial Position (Balance Sheet)

Assets (Current + Non-current) = Liabilities (Current + Non-current) + Equity.

7. Worked Comprehensive Example

Scenario: On 1 Jan equipment bought for RM50,000 cash. Residual value RM5,000, useful life 9 years. On 31 Dec prepare the depreciation adjusting entry and show how it appears in the financial statements.

Depreciable amount = 50,000 − 5,000 = 45,000
Annual depreciation = 45,000 / 9 = RM5,000

Journal:
Dr Depreciation Expense 5,000
   Cr Accumulated Depreciation – Equipment 5,000

Income Statement: Depreciation Expense RM5,000 (reduces profit)
Statement of Financial Position:
Equipment 50,000
Less: Accumulated Depreciation (5,000)
Carrying amount RM45,000

8. Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Forgetting that adjusting entries never involve Cash.
  • Confusing Prepaid Rent (asset) with Rent Expense.
  • Writing “Depreciation” instead of “Accumulated Depreciation” on the credit side.
  • Mixing up debit/credit rules for expenses and revenues.
  • Calculating depreciation on cost instead of (cost − residual value).

9. Exam Tips & Strategy

  • Always start Conceptual Framework questions with the exact definition from the Framework.
  • Show all workings for depreciation and adjusting entries — method marks are given.
  • Prepare a quick T-account or equation check if you have time.
  • Label every journal entry with a short narration.
  • Practise full cycles (transactions → adjustments → statements) under timed conditions.
