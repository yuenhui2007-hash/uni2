BTW1042 – Business Law

Degree-level: sources of law, contract formation & vitiation, negligence analysis, agency, business structures

1. Malaysian Legal System for Business Students

The Federal Constitution is supreme; inconsistent legislation is void to the extent of inconsistency. Business law draws on statute (e.g. Contracts Act 1950, Companies Act 2016, Partnership Act 1961) and common-law/equitable principles received where applicable and suitable to local circumstances. Judicial precedent (stare decisis) binds lower courts to higher-court rulings on like points of law. Hierarchy (simplified): Federal Court → Court of Appeal → High Court → subordinate courts.

2. Contract Formation — Analytical Structure

A contract is an agreement enforceable by law. Formation analysis should systematically test: offer, acceptance, consideration, intention to create legal relations, capacity, certainty, and legality of object — then consider vitiating factors.

Offer vs invitation to treat

Offer: a definite proposal made with the intention that it will become binding upon acceptance.
Invitation to treat: an invitation to make offers / negotiate (display of goods, ordinary advertisements, auction calls for bids). Not itself an offer.

Acceptance must mirror the offer (unqualified). A purported acceptance that introduces new terms is a counter-offer, rejecting the original offer. Acceptance must generally be communicated; silence is not acceptance absent exceptional circumstances.

Consideration and intention

Consideration is value in the eyes of the law moving from the promisee; sufficiency is required, adequacy is not. Past consideration is generally ineffective. Commercial agreements carry a presumption of intention to create legal relations; social/domestic agreements carry the opposite presumption — both rebuttable by evidence.

Vitiating factors

Misrepresentation (innocent, negligent, fraudulent), mistake, coercion, undue influence, and illegality can render contracts voidable or void and support remedies (rescission, damages) depending on the factor and statutory framework. Exam answers must identify the factor, state the legal test, apply facts, and state the consequence.

3. Negligence — Structured Proof

  1. Duty of care — relationship or circumstances imposing a legal duty; for novel cases, foreseeability, proximity, and whether imposition is fair, just and reasonable
  2. Breach — failure to meet the standard of the reasonable person / reasonably competent professional
  3. Causation — factual (“but for”) and legal causation (no novus actus interveniens breaking the chain)
  4. Remoteness — type of damage reasonably foreseeable

Professionals (including accountants) owe duties to clients; third-party liability for pure economic loss is more constrained and depends on assumption of responsibility and reliance. Defences include contributory negligence and voluntary assumption of risk.

4. Agency

Agency enables an agent to affect the principal’s legal relations with third parties. Actual authority (express/implied) vs apparent authority (estoppel from the principal’s representation). Agent duties: obedience to lawful instructions, care and skill, no conflict/secret profit, account. Principal duties: agreed remuneration and indemnity.
