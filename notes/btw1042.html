Definition bank — business law

Definition

Offer

A clear expression of willingness to be bound on specified terms, made with the intention that it becomes binding as soon as the other party accepts.

Example: “I will sell you this car for RM40,000” said to a specific buyer.
Definition

Invitation to treat

An invitation to others to make an offer; not itself an offer capable of immediate acceptance creating a contract.

Example: Goods on supermarket shelves; most advertisements; auction calls for bids.
Definition

Acceptance

Unqualified agreement to all terms of the offer, communicated to the offeror (subject to limited exceptions).

Example: “I accept your offer to buy at RM40,000” — not “I accept at RM38,000” (that is a counter-offer).
Definition

Consideration

Something of value given in exchange for a promise (benefit to one party or detriment to the other). Must be sufficient, need not be adequate; past consideration is generally not valid.

Example: Paying RM1 for a contract option can be valid consideration even if the option seems “cheap”.
Definition

Negligence

Breach of a duty of care owed to the claimant, causing recoverable damage that is not too remote.

Elements: duty → breach → causation → remoteness (damage).
Definition

Separate legal personality

A company is a legal person distinct from its shareholders and directors (Salomon principle). Company owns assets and incurs liabilities in its own name.

Example: Creditor of Sdn Bhd sues the company, not the shareholders (subject to rare veil-lifting).

1. Malaysian legal system (business focus)

Federal Constitution is supreme. Sources: written law (Constitution, Acts, subsidiary legislation) and unwritten law (common law/equity as received and suitable locally; precedent; custom). Hierarchy: Federal Court → Court of Appeal → High Court → Sessions/Magistrates. Stare decisis: lower courts follow higher courts on like points of law.

Key statutes for this unit: Contracts Act 1950, Partnership Act 1961, Companies Act 2016.

3. Tort of negligence

Four elements — all required:

  1. Duty of care — foreseeability, proximity, fair/just/reasonable (novel cases)
  2. Breach — below standard of reasonable person / reasonably competent professional
  3. Causation — factual “but for” + legal causation
  4. Remoteness — type of loss reasonably foreseeable

Negligent misstatement / pure economic loss: special relationship, assumption of responsibility, reasonable reliance (accountants advising clients).

  1. Duty of care — relationship / foreseeability / proximity.
  2. Breach — below the standard of a reasonably competent professional.
  3. Causation — but-for + legal causation.
  4. Remoteness — type of loss reasonably foreseeable.
  5. Conclude on liability and mention possible defences if relevant.

4. Agency

Agent can bind principal to third parties. Actual authority (express/implied) vs apparent authority (principal’s representation + third-party reliance). Agent duties: follow instructions, care and skill, avoid conflict, account. Principal: pay and indemnify as agreed.

6. IRAC drills (memorise structure)

Problem: Ali offers car RM40,000. Ben: “I accept if spare tyre included.” Ali silent.
I: Is there a binding contract?
R: Acceptance must be unqualified; conditional acceptance = counter-offer rejecting original offer; silence ≠ acceptance.
A: Ben introduced a new term → counter-offer. Ali did not accept the counter-offer.
C: No contract formed.
Negligence sketch: Accountant gives careless tax advice; client relies; penalties imposed.
Duty (client relationship) → Breach (below competent standard) → Causation (but for advice) → Remoteness (financial loss foreseeable) → liability likely subject to defences.

9. Consumer protection (Malaysia — awareness)

Consumer statutes and unfair contract terms regimes protect consumers in supply of goods/services. Businesses must not engage in misleading conduct. Exam level: identify consumer relationship and that statutory rights may limit exclusion clauses.

10. Misrepresentation (contract vitiation detail)

  • Fraudulent: knowing/without belief in truth / reckless
  • Negligent: carelessly made where duty of care exists
  • Innocent: false but honestly believed

Remedies differ (rescission, damages). Silence is not usually misrepresentation unless duty to disclose or half-truth.

11. Dispute resolution

Litigation vs ADR (negotiation, mediation, arbitration). Business contracts often include arbitration clauses. Know when each is appropriate (cost, speed, confidentiality, finality).

Exam: State the rule before applying facts. Incomplete IRAC = mid-band only.

16. Model IRAC — negligence full sample

Facts: Auditor carelessly misses fraud indicators; lender relies on audited accounts; loan loss 500,000.
I: Is the auditor liable in negligence to the lender?
R: Duty (special relationship / assumption of responsibility), breach (competent auditor standard), causation, remoteness of pure economic loss.
A: Apply whether the auditor knew the lender would rely; whether audit fell below standard; whether loss was a foreseeable type.
C: Liability depends on proximity/reliance facts; if duty established and breach proved, damages for foreseeable loss likely.

Mini IRAC — counter-offer
Offer: sell bike RM500. Reply: “I’ll pay RM450.” Offeror refuses. Buyer then says “OK, RM500.”
I: Is there a contract at 500?
R: Counter-offer rejects original offer; original cannot be accepted later unless renewed.
A: RM450 was counter-offer; original offer dead; later “accept” is a new offer needing acceptance.
C: No contract unless offeror accepts the renewed offer.
