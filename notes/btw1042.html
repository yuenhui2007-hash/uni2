1. Malaysian legal system (business focus)

Federal Constitution is supreme. Sources: written law (Constitution, Acts, subsidiary legislation) and unwritten law (common law/equity as received and suitable locally; precedent; custom). Hierarchy: Federal Court → Court of Appeal → High Court → Sessions/Magistrates. Stare decisis: lower courts follow higher courts on like points of law.

Key statutes for this unit: Contracts Act 1950, Partnership Act 1961, Companies Act 2016.

2. Contract law — formation to vitiation

Enforceable agreement requires systematic proof of elements:

  1. Offer
  2. Acceptance
  3. Consideration
  4. Intention to create legal relations
  5. Capacity
  6. Certainty of terms
  7. Lawful object
Offer: definite proposal with intention to be bound on acceptance.
Invitation to treat: willingness to negotiate (shop display, ordinary ads, auction call for bids) — not an offer.
Acceptance: absolute and unqualified (mirror image). New terms = counter-offer (rejects original offer).
Consideration: value in law from the promisee; sufficient not adequate; past consideration generally invalid.

Intention: commercial agreements presumed legal intention; domestic/social presumed not — both rebuttable.

Vitiating factors: misrepresentation (innocent/negligent/fraudulent), mistake, coercion, undue influence, illegality → void or voidable + remedies (rescission/damages) as applicable.

3. Tort of negligence

Four elements — all required:

  1. Duty of care — foreseeability, proximity, fair/just/reasonable (novel cases)
  2. Breach — below standard of reasonable person / reasonably competent professional
  3. Causation — factual “but for” + legal causation
  4. Remoteness — type of loss reasonably foreseeable

Negligent misstatement / pure economic loss: special relationship, assumption of responsibility, reasonable reliance (accountants advising clients).

4. Agency

Agent can bind principal to third parties. Actual authority (express/implied) vs apparent authority (principal’s representation + third-party reliance). Agent duties: follow instructions, care and skill, avoid conflict, account. Principal: pay and indemnify as agreed.

6. IRAC drills (memorise structure)

Problem: Ali offers car RM40,000. Ben: “I accept if spare tyre included.” Ali silent.
I: Is there a binding contract?
R: Acceptance must be unqualified; conditional acceptance = counter-offer rejecting original offer; silence ≠ acceptance.
A: Ben introduced a new term → counter-offer. Ali did not accept the counter-offer.
C: No contract formed.
Negligence sketch: Accountant gives careless tax advice; client relies; penalties imposed.
Duty (client relationship) → Breach (below competent standard) → Causation (but for advice) → Remoteness (financial loss foreseeable) → liability likely subject to defences.

9. Consumer protection (Malaysia — awareness)

Consumer statutes and unfair contract terms regimes protect consumers in supply of goods/services. Businesses must not engage in misleading conduct. Exam level: identify consumer relationship and that statutory rights may limit exclusion clauses.

10. Misrepresentation (contract vitiation detail)

  • Fraudulent: knowing/without belief in truth / reckless
  • Negligent: carelessly made where duty of care exists
  • Innocent: false but honestly believed

Remedies differ (rescission, damages). Silence is not usually misrepresentation unless duty to disclose or half-truth.

11. Dispute resolution

Litigation vs ADR (negotiation, mediation, arbitration). Business contracts often include arbitration clauses. Know when each is appropriate (cost, speed, confidentiality, finality).

Exam: State the rule before applying facts. Incomplete IRAC = mid-band only.
