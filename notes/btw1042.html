BTW1042 – Business Law

Advanced notes: doctrinal tests, IRAC application, negligence economic loss, corporate personality

1. Legal Method for Business Problems

Malaysian business law is a hybrid of statute and common-law reasoning. Answers must identify the governing source (e.g. Contracts Act 1950 ss. on proposal/acceptance/consideration; Companies Act 2016 on separate personality and limited liability), state the doctrinal test, apply facts with close attention to timing and communication, and conclude on enforceability or liability. IRAC is not optional formatting — it is the assessment structure.

2. Contract — Formation Pathologies

Offer requires intention to be bound on acceptance and sufficient certainty of terms. Invitations to treat (shop display, ordinary ads, tenders as invitations unless clearly offers) protect the inviter from unintended multilateral acceptances. Cross-offers do not form a contract without a moment of consensus.

Acceptance must be final and unqualified. Battle-of-forms scenarios turn on last shot / dominant offer analysis as taught. Communication rules: instantaneous communication effective when received; postal acceptance rule (if applicable to the facts and not ousted) makes acceptance effective on posting — know exceptions (not correctly addressed; instantaneous methods; offeror prescribes method).

Consideration: executory or executed; must move from promisee; sufficiency ≠ adequacy; past consideration generally bad unless requested act with implied contemporaneous promise (limited exception as taught). Performance of existing public/contractual duty is problematic consideration unless exceeding the duty or conferring practical benefit (modern common-law developments — apply only if in syllabus).

Intention: commercial presumption vs domestic presumption; rebuttal with explicit wording (“subject to contract”, honour clauses).

Vitiation: misrepresentation (type drives remedy), duress/coercion, undue influence, mistake (common/mutual/unilateral — narrow), illegality and public policy. Map each to void vs voidable and available remedies.

3. Negligence — Including Pure Economic Loss

Duty, breach, causation (factual + legal), remoteness. Professional standard: Bolam-type reasonably competent peer practice, qualified by logical basis of that practice where taught. Pure economic loss for negligent misstatement requires special relationship / assumption of responsibility / reasonable reliance (Hedley Byrne line as received). Accountants’ exposure to third parties is fact-sensitive — engagement letter disclaimers and identified user groups matter.

4. Agency and Attribution

Actual authority (express/implied incidental) vs apparent authority (representation by principal, reliance by third party). Undisclosed principal doctrine and ratification (timing, knowledge, capacity) if in syllabus. Vicarious liability and corporate attribution are distinct from agency but often examined together in employment/commercial scenarios.

5. Business Organisations — Legal Architecture

Salomon principle: company is a separate legal person; shareholders’ liability limited to amount unpaid on shares (CA 2016). Veil-piercing remains exceptional (façade, fraud, specific statutory routes). Partnership: default unlimited joint and several liability; agency of partners for partnership business. Choice of form is a risk-allocation and governance decision, not merely “prestige”.
