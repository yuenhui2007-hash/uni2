BTW1042 – Business Law

Detailed notes: Malaysian legal system, contract law, tort of negligence, agency, business structures.

1. Unit Overview

Focuses on practical legal principles that affect business decisions in Malaysia. You learn how to form enforceable contracts, manage legal risk, avoid negligence liability, and choose appropriate business structures. Use the IRAC method (Issue–Rule–Application–Conclusion) for problem questions.

2. Malaysian Legal System

  • Federal Constitution is the supreme law of Malaysia.
  • Sources: written law (Constitution, Acts of Parliament, subsidiary legislation) and unwritten law (common law, equity, judicial precedent, customs).
  • Court hierarchy (simplified): Federal Court → Court of Appeal → High Court → Sessions Court → Magistrates’ Court.
  • Doctrine of binding precedent (stare decisis) applies.

3. Contract Law (Contracts Act 1950)

Essential Elements of a Valid Contract

  1. Offer
  2. Acceptance
  3. Consideration
  4. Intention to create legal relations
  5. Capacity
  6. Free consent (no coercion, undue influence, fraud, misrepresentation, mistake)
  7. Lawful object and consideration
  8. Certainty of terms
Offer – a definite proposal made with the intention that it shall become binding upon acceptance.
Invitation to treat – an expression of willingness to negotiate (e.g. price tags, advertisements, auctioneer calling for bids). Not an offer.
Acceptance – must be absolute and unqualified (mirror-image rule). A conditional acceptance is a counter-offer that rejects the original offer.
Consideration – something of value given by each party. Must be sufficient but need not be adequate. Past consideration is generally not valid.

Vitiating Factors

  • Misrepresentation (innocent, negligent, fraudulent)
  • Mistake
  • Coercion / Duress
  • Undue influence
  • Illegality

4. Tort of Negligence

Four elements must be proved:

  1. Duty of care – defendant owed a duty to the plaintiff
  2. Breach of duty – fell below the standard of the reasonable person / reasonably competent professional
  3. Causation – the breach caused the damage (factual and legal causation)
  4. Damage not too remote – type of loss was reasonably foreseeable

Professional advisers (accountants, auditors, lawyers) can owe a duty of care to clients and, in limited circumstances, to third parties who rely on their advice.

5. Agency

  • Relationship where an agent is authorised to act on behalf of a principal and can bind the principal in contracts with third parties.
  • Creation: express, implied, ratification, necessity, estoppel (apparent authority).
  • Agent’s duties: follow instructions, exercise reasonable care and skill, avoid conflict of interest, account for money.
  • Principal’s duties: pay agreed remuneration, indemnify the agent.

6. Business Structures

Feature Sole Prop Partnership Sdn Bhd
Separate legal personality No Generally No Yes
Liability Unlimited Unlimited (joint & several) Limited
Key statute — Partnership Act 1961 Companies Act 2016

7. Exam Tips

  • Always use IRAC for problem questions.
  • State the relevant section of the Contracts Act 1950 or Companies Act 2016 when possible.
  • Distinguish clearly between offer and invitation to treat.
  • For negligence, go through all four elements systematically.
  • Compare business structures on liability, continuity, regulation and raising capital.
