MGW1010 – Introduction to Management

Complete exam-prep: functions, theories + critique, leadership, ethics, essay structure

1. Management· 2. Classical· 3. Behavioural· 4. Contingency· 5. Leadership· 6. Ethics· 7. Essays

1. What management is

Management = planning, organising, leading and controlling resources to achieve goals effectively (right goals) and efficiently (minimal waste).
  • Planning: goals, strategy, plans
  • Organising: structure, roles, authority, coordination
  • Leading: motivation, communication, leadership, teams
  • Controlling: measure, compare, correct

2. Classical approaches + critique

Scientific Management (Taylor): scientific study of tasks, standards, selection/training, incentives. Critique: treats workers as machines; ignores social needs; weak fit for knowledge work.

Administrative theory (Fayol): functions + principles (unity of command, scalar chain…). Principles are guidelines, not laws.

Bureaucracy (Weber): hierarchy, rules, impersonality, merit. Strength: predictability at scale. Critique: red tape, rigidity, goal displacement.

3. Behavioural approaches

Hawthorne: social factors and attention affect productivity.

  • Maslow: physiological → safety → social → esteem → self-actualisation
  • McGregor: Theory X (dislike work, need control) vs Theory Y (self-direction possible)
  • Herzberg: hygiene factors prevent dissatisfaction; motivators create satisfaction (achievement, recognition, work itself, responsibility, growth)

4. Systems and contingency

Contingency approach: no one best way; effective practice depends on situation (environment, technology, size, people, strategy).

Systems view: organisation as interrelated parts exchanging with the environment (open system). Mechanistic structures fit stable environments; organic structures fit dynamic ones (as taught).

5. Leadership

Transactional: rewards for performance; management by exception — maintains standards.
Transformational: vision, intellectual stimulation, individualised consideration, idealised influence — drives change commitment.

Neither is always best: match style to task, followers, and need for change. Contingency theories (e.g. Fiedler, path–goal if in syllabus) formalise “it depends”.

6. Ethics and CSR

Frameworks: utilitarian (greatest good), rights, justice, care. Apply more than one lens to the same case for higher marks. CSR/ESG: responsibilities beyond short-term profit; watch greenwashing; governance must back claims.

7. Essay structure (use every time)

  1. Define key terms
  2. Explain theory / model
  3. Apply to organisation or scenario
  4. Critique assumptions, evidence, modern relevance
  5. Conclude with managerial implication
Question type: “Critically evaluate Scientific Management for remote knowledge teams.”
Define Taylorism → explain principles → apply to remote coding/design work → critique (autonomy, creativity, monitoring toxicity) → conclude hybrid process discipline + Theory Y trust.
Exam: Pure description without critique = mid marks. Always evaluate.
