1. What management is

Management = planning, organising, leading and controlling resources to achieve goals effectively (right goals) and efficiently (minimal waste).
  • Planning: goals, strategy, plans
  • Organising: structure, roles, authority, coordination
  • Leading: motivation, communication, leadership, teams
  • Controlling: measure, compare, correct

2. Classical approaches + critique

Essay marksDefine → explain → apply → critique → conclude. Description without critique = mid marks.

Scientific Management (Taylor): scientific study of tasks, standards, selection/training, incentives. Critique: treats workers as machines; ignores social needs; weak fit for knowledge work.

Administrative theory (Fayol): functions + principles (unity of command, scalar chain…). Principles are guidelines, not laws.

Bureaucracy (Weber): hierarchy, rules, impersonality, merit. Strength: predictability at scale. Critique: red tape, rigidity, goal displacement.

  1. Define the theory in one precise sentence.
  2. Explain 2–3 core principles with labels.
  3. Apply to the organisation or scenario in the question.
  4. Critique assumptions, evidence, and modern fit.
  5. Close with a managerial implication.
Self-check — Classical theory
  1. One strength and one weakness of Scientific Management.
  2. What problem does bureaucracy create at scale?
  3. Why do classical “one best way” claims fail for knowledge work?

3. Behavioural approaches

Hawthorne: social factors and attention affect productivity.

  • Maslow: physiological → safety → social → esteem → self-actualisation
  • McGregor: Theory X (dislike work, need control) vs Theory Y (self-direction possible)
  • Herzberg: hygiene factors prevent dissatisfaction; motivators create satisfaction (achievement, recognition, work itself, responsibility, growth)

4. Systems and contingency

Contingency approach: no one best way; effective practice depends on situation (environment, technology, size, people, strategy).

Systems view: organisation as interrelated parts exchanging with the environment (open system). Mechanistic structures fit stable environments; organic structures fit dynamic ones (as taught).

Memory aidContingency = “it depends.” Fit practice to environment, technology, people, and strategy — not a universal formula.
Self-check — Contingency & systems
  1. State the contingency approach in one sentence.
  2. Mechanistic vs organic structures — which fits a stable environment?

5. Leadership

Transactional: rewards for performance; management by exception — maintains standards.
Transformational: vision, intellectual stimulation, individualised consideration, idealised influence — drives change commitment.

Neither is always best: match style to task, followers, and need for change. Contingency theories (e.g. Fiedler, path–goal if in syllabus) formalise “it depends”.

Do notWrite “transformational is always better.” Match style to task, followers, and need for change.
Self-check — Leadership
  1. Transactional vs transformational — one feature each.
  2. When might transactional leadership be appropriate?

6. Ethics and CSR

Frameworks: utilitarian (greatest good), rights, justice, care. Apply more than one lens to the same case for higher marks. CSR/ESG: responsibilities beyond short-term profit; watch greenwashing; governance must back claims.

7. Essay structure (use every time)

  1. Define key terms
  2. Explain theory / model
  3. Apply to organisation or scenario
  4. Critique assumptions, evidence, modern relevance
  5. Conclude with managerial implication
Question type: “Critically evaluate Scientific Management for remote knowledge teams.”
Define Taylorism → explain principles → apply to remote coding/design work → critique (autonomy, creativity, monitoring toxicity) → conclude hybrid process discipline + Theory Y trust.
Exam: Pure description without critique = mid marks. Always evaluate.

9. Motivation theories — quick apply

  • Maslow: lower needs before higher; workplace: pay/safety before belonging/esteem/growth
  • Herzberg: hygiene prevents dissatisfaction; motivators create satisfaction
  • McGregor: Theory X control vs Theory Y self-direction assumptions shape management style
Remote software team demotivated despite high pay → hygiene may be OK; motivators (autonomy, meaningful work, recognition) likely weak → redesign jobs / leadership, not only bonuses.

10. Essay skeleton you can reuse

  1. Introduction with definition + thesis
  2. Theory explanation with labelled points
  3. Application to the case/organisation
  4. Critique (assumptions, evidence, context fit)
  5. Conclusion with managerial implication
Self-check — Essay readiness
  1. Can you critique Taylor for knowledge work in 4 sentences?
  2. Contingency approach in one sentence?
  3. When is transactional leadership appropriate?

11. POLC in practice — short cases

Planning failure: ambitious sales target with no capacity plan → stockouts.
Organising failure: unclear reporting lines → duplicated work.
Leading failure: no feedback / recognition → disengagement.
Controlling failure: no KPIs until year-end → surprises too late to correct.

12. Culture and structure (link to contingency)

Structure shapes behaviour; culture shapes what is valued. A rigid hierarchy may fit compliance-heavy work; innovation units often need flatter structure and psychological safety. Contingency thinking asks which fit serves strategy in this environment.

Self-check
  1. Give one example of planning and one of controlling in a retail store.
  2. Why might Theory Y assumptions fit creative teams better than Theory X?

13. Ethics decision mini-framework

  1. Identify stakeholders and harms/benefits.
  2. Apply utilitarian, rights, and justice lenses briefly.
  3. Check legal minimum vs ethical standard.
  4. Recommend action + monitoring (so CSR is not only rhetoric).
