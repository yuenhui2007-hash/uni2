MGW1010 – Introduction to Management

Advanced notes: critical theory evaluation, contingency fit, leadership evidence limits, ethics application

1. Management Beyond the Four Functions

Planning–organising–leading–controlling is a heuristic, not a complete theory of the firm. Contemporary management confronts uncertainty, bounded rationality, institutional pressures and power. Exam essays that only list functions without tension (exploration vs exploitation; control vs autonomy; efficiency vs resilience) remain at pass-level description.

2. Classical Theory — Critical Reading

Taylorism: productivity gains via standardisation and incentive alignment; labour process critique (deskilling, managerial monopoly on knowledge); poor external validity for non-routine cognitive work and professional services.

Fayol: universal principles under-specified contingent conditions; still shapes vocabulary of coordination.

Weberian bureaucracy: technical superiority for stable large-scale administration; dysfunctions — trained incapacity, goal displacement, bureaucratic ritualism. Post-bureaucratic forms trade formalisation for flexibility at the cost of role ambiguity.

3. Behavioural Theories — Boundary Conditions

Maslow: hierarchical, culturally biased empirical support; useful as vocabulary not predictive law. McGregor: Theory X/Y as self-fulfilling managerial ideologies. Herzberg: dual-factor structure debated; job enrichment implications remain influential in job design (autonomy, feedback, skill variety — link to Hackman–Oldham if taught).

4. Contingency and Systems

Structural contingency: mechanistic structures fit stable environments; organic structures fit dynamic ones (Burns & Stalker tradition). Technology, size and strategy are additional contingency variables. Systems theory: organisations as open systems with feedback; ignores politics unless paired with stakeholder/resource-dependence perspectives.

5. Leadership Research Literacy

Trait → behavioural → contingency (Fiedler, path–goal) → neo-charismatic/transformational. Transformational leadership correlates with positive outcomes in many studies but faces endogeneity, common-method bias and “heroic leadership” critique. Distributed and shared leadership models respond to knowledge-intensive contexts. Match style to task structure, follower competence and change intensity.

6. Ethics as Analysis, Not Slogan

Apply utilitarian, deontological, justice and care lenses to the same case and show divergent recommendations. CSR/ESG: instrumental vs normative rationales; greenwashing risk; governance mechanisms (boards, disclosure, incentives) that make commitments credible.

Essay band criteria: theory accuracy → applied illustration → critique (assumptions, evidence, context) → managerial implication. Description without critique plateaus at mid marks.
