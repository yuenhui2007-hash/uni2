MGW1010 – Introduction to Management

Detailed notes: management functions, classical & behavioural theories, leadership, ethics, contingency approach, contemporary issues.

1. What is Management?

Management – the process of planning, organising, leading and controlling resources to achieve organisational goals effectively and efficiently.
  • Effectiveness – doing the right things (goal attainment)
  • Efficiency – doing things right (minimising waste of resources)

2. The Four Functions of Management

  1. Planning – setting goals and deciding how to achieve them
  2. Organising – arranging and structuring work
  3. Leading – working with and through people (motivation, communication, leadership style)
  4. Controlling – monitoring performance, comparing with goals, taking corrective action

3. Classical Approaches

  • Scientific Management (Frederick Taylor) – efficiency through scientific study of work, time-and-motion studies, standardisation, piece-rate pay. Criticised for treating workers as machines.
  • Administrative Theory (Henri Fayol) – 14 principles of management; identified the functions of management.
  • Bureaucracy (Max Weber) – hierarchical structure, clear rules and procedures, impersonal relationships, merit-based advancement. Can become rigid and slow.

4. Behavioural / Human Relations Approaches

  • Hawthorne Studies – social factors and employee attitudes significantly affect productivity.
  • Maslow’s Hierarchy of Needs – physiological → safety → social → esteem → self-actualisation.
  • McGregor’s Theory X and Theory Y – Theory X assumes workers dislike work and need control; Theory Y assumes workers can be self-motivated and seek responsibility.
  • Herzberg’s Two-Factor Theory – Hygiene factors (prevent dissatisfaction) vs Motivators (create satisfaction).

5. Contemporary Approaches

  • Systems approach – organisation as interrelated parts interacting with the external environment.
  • Contingency approach – “it depends”; there is no one best way. Effective management depends on the situation (environment, technology, size, people).

6. Leadership

  • Autocratic, Democratic, Laissez-faire styles
  • Transactional leadership – exchange-based (rewards for performance)
  • Transformational leadership – inspires followers through vision, intellectual stimulation, individualised consideration and idealised influence
  • Servant leadership, Authentic leadership

7. Ethics, CSR & Contemporary Issues

  • Ethical frameworks: utilitarian, rights, justice, care
  • Corporate Social Responsibility (CSR) and ESG
  • Globalisation and cultural diversity (Hofstede’s dimensions)
  • Digital transformation, remote/hybrid work, sustainability

8. Exam Tips

  • Be prepared to critically evaluate theories (strengths, weaknesses, modern relevance).
  • Use organisational examples to illustrate points.
  • Link classical ideas to contemporary issues.
  • Structure essays: define → explain theory → apply → critique → conclude.
  • For ethics questions, apply a clear framework.
