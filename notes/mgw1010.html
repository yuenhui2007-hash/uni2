MGW1010 – Introduction to Management

Critical introduction to management theory, practice, ethics and contemporary issues.

1. Unit Overview

This unit questions what management is, what managers actually do, and how one becomes an effective manager. It explores the effects of management practices on employees and weaves in globalisation, cultural diversity, ethics and social responsibility throughout.

2. Key Definitions

Management
The process of planning, organising, leading and controlling the use of resources to achieve organisational goals effectively and efficiently.
Effectiveness vs Efficiency
Effectiveness = doing the right things (goal attainment).
Efficiency = doing things right (minimising resource waste).
Planning
Setting goals and deciding how to achieve them.
Organising
Arranging and structuring work to accomplish organisational goals.
Leading
Working with and through people to accomplish goals; includes motivation, communication and leadership style.
Controlling
Monitoring performance, comparing it with goals, and taking corrective action as needed.

3. Core Concepts

Classical Approaches

  • Scientific Management (Taylor) – efficiency through scientific study of work, time-and-motion studies, standardisation.
  • Administrative Theory (Fayol) – 14 principles of management; functions of management.
  • Bureaucracy (Weber) – hierarchical structure, clear rules, impersonal relationships, merit-based advancement.

Behavioural / Human Relations Approaches

  • Hawthorne Studies – social factors and employee attitudes affect productivity.
  • Maslow’s Hierarchy of Needs
  • McGregor’s Theory X and Theory Y
  • Herzberg’s Two-Factor Theory (motivators vs hygiene factors)

Contemporary Themes

  • Systems approach – organisation as interrelated parts interacting with the environment.
  • Contingency approach – “it depends”; no one best way.
  • Globalisation and cultural diversity (Hofstede’s dimensions).
  • Ethics and Corporate Social Responsibility (CSR).
  • Sustainability and ESG considerations.
  • Digital transformation and remote/hybrid work.

Leadership Styles

  • Autocratic, Democratic, Laissez-faire
  • Transactional vs Transformational leadership
  • Servant leadership, Authentic leadership

4. Application Examples

Theory X vs Theory Y in practice
Manager A assumes employees dislike work and need close supervision (Theory X) → uses strict rules and controls.
Manager B assumes employees can be self-motivated and seek responsibility (Theory Y) → uses empowerment and participation.
Contingency thinking
A start-up in a rapidly changing tech environment may need a flexible, organic structure, while a large manufacturing firm may benefit from more mechanistic controls.

5. Exam Tips

  • Be prepared to critically evaluate theories – strengths, weaknesses and modern relevance.
  • Use real or hypothetical organisational examples to illustrate points.
  • Link classical ideas to contemporary issues (e.g., does Taylorism still exist in gig work?).
  • For ethics questions, apply frameworks (utilitarian, rights, justice, care).
  • Structure essays clearly: define → explain theory → apply → critique → conclude.
