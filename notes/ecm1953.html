Definition bank — economics

Definition

Demand

The quantity of a good consumers are willing and able to buy at each price, other things equal (ceteris paribus).

Law of demand: as price rises, quantity demanded falls (movement along the curve).
Definition

Supply

The quantity producers are willing and able to sell at each price, ceteris paribus.

Law of supply: as price rises, quantity supplied rises (movement along the curve).
Definition

Equilibrium

Price and quantity where quantity demanded equals quantity supplied; no shortage or surplus at that price.

Example: If price is above equilibrium → surplus → pressure for price to fall.
Definition

Price elasticity of demand (PED)

% change in quantity demanded divided by % change in price. Measures responsiveness of demand to price.

|PED| > 1 elastic · < 1 inelastic · = 1 unit elastic.
Definition

Aggregate demand (AD)

Total planned spending on domestic output: C + I + G + (X − M).

Example: Tax cut raises disposable income → C↑ → AD shifts right.
Definition

Opportunity cost

Value of the next best alternative forgone when a choice is made.

Example: Time spent studying economics cannot be used to earn wages at a part-time job.
Key concept

Shift vs movement

Movement along the curve: caused by a change in the good’s own price.
Shift of the curve: caused by non-price determinants (income, tastes, input costs, etc.).

Example: Coffee price falls → movement along demand for coffee. Health report raises preference for coffee → demand curve shifts right.

1. Economic way of thinking

Opportunity cost = value of the next-best alternative forgone. Rational choice compares marginal benefit and marginal cost.

PPF: points on curve = efficient; inside = inefficient; outside = unattainable given current resources/tech. Growth shifts PPF outward.

2. Demand, supply, equilibrium

Diagram marksAxes titles, curve labels, equilibrium markers, and arrows for shifts — incomplete diagrams lose easy marks.

Law of demand: P↑ → Qd↓ (ceteris paribus). Law of supply: P↑ → Qs↑. Equilibrium: Qd=Qs.

Own-price change → movement along curve. Other determinants → shift of curve (income, tastes, related goods, expectations, input costs, technology, taxes, number of firms/buyers).

Demand ↑ (shift right), supply fixed → P*↑ and Q*↑.
Supply ↑, demand fixed → P*↓ and Q*↑.
Both shift: one of P or Q may be ambiguous — say so.
Do notConfuse a movement along a curve (own-price change) with a shift of the curve (other determinants).
Memory aidRight = rise in demand/supply. For demand: right means higher Q at each P. Always state what happens to P* and Q*.

  2. Input costs fall. Supply shifts which way? Effect on P* and Q*?

3. Elasticity

PED = (%ΔQd)/(%ΔP)

|PED|>1 elastic; =1 unit; <1 inelastic. Price rise increases TR if inelastic; decreases TR if elastic. Determinants: substitutes, necessity, budget share, time.

P: 10→12 (+20%); Qd: 200→160 (−20%) → PED = −1 → unit elastic; TR unchanged (10×200=2000; 12×160=1920 ≈ check midpoint method if required).
  1. Compute %ΔQd and %ΔP (or use midpoint if required).
  2. PED = %ΔQd / %ΔP (usually negative; use absolute value to classify).
  3. |PED| > 1 elastic; < 1 inelastic; = 1 unit.
  4. Link to total revenue: price rise raises TR if inelastic; lowers TR if elastic.
Self-check — Elasticity
  1. P from 10 to 12, Qd from 100 to 80. Rough |PED|?
  2. If demand is inelastic, should a firm raise or cut price to raise TR?

5. Macro: AD–AS and policy

AD = C + I + G + (X − M)

AD↑ (e.g. higher G) → short run: higher Y and higher P along SRAS. LRAS at potential output in classical long-run benchmark.

Monetary policy: interest rates / money (Bank Negara). Lower i → I and durable C↑ → AD↑.
Fiscal policy: G and taxes. Expansionary: ↑G or ↓T → AD↑. Lags and crowding-out may limit impact.

Policy answersName the curve that shifts, state short-run effects on Y and P, then give one limitation (lags, crowding-out, capacity).

8. Elasticity applications

P 20→24 (+20%); Qd 500→450 (−10%) → PED = −0.5 inelastic
TR was 10,000; now 10,800 → TR rose with price increase (consistent with inelastic demand)
  • Necessities with few substitutes → more inelastic
  • Longer time horizon → more elastic
  • Luxury / many substitutes → more elastic

9. Perfect competition vs monopoly snapshot

Competition
Many firms, price taker, P = MR = MC at optimum; long-run normal profit if free entry
Monopoly
One firm, downward demand, MR < P; sets MR = MC then reads P on demand; barriers sustain profit

10. Policy transmission mini-cases

Case A: Cut in income tax → disposable income ↑ → C ↑ → AD right → Y↑ P↑ in SR
Case B: Central bank raises rates → I and durable C ↓ → AD left → Y↓ P pressure eases
Case C: Near full capacity + big G increase → mostly P↑, limited Y↑

11. Consumer and producer surplus (awareness)

At equilibrium, consumer surplus is the area above price and below demand; producer surplus is below price and above supply. A binding price ceiling below equilibrium creates shortage and usually reduces total surplus (deadweight loss) — draw and label if asked.

Do notForget to state shortage/surplus direction when price controls are set above or below equilibrium.

12. Macro identities and gaps

  • If actual output < potential → negative output gap → unemployment above natural rate (in standard models)
  • If actual output > potential → positive gap → inflationary pressure
  • Stabilisation policy aims to smooth fluctuations — not to “abolish” the business cycle entirely
Economy in deep recession: expansionary fiscal or monetary policy shifts AD right → higher Y toward potential; inflation risk lower than at full capacity.
