ECM1953 – Principles of Economics

1. Economic way of thinking

Opportunity cost = value of the next-best alternative forgone. Rational choice compares marginal benefit and marginal cost.

PPF: points on curve = efficient; inside = inefficient; outside = unattainable given current resources/tech. Growth shifts PPF outward.

2. Demand, supply, equilibrium

Law of demand: P↑ → Qd↓ (ceteris paribus). Law of supply: P↑ → Qs↑. Equilibrium: Qd=Qs.

Own-price change → movement along curve. Other determinants → shift of curve (income, tastes, related goods, expectations, input costs, technology, taxes, number of firms/buyers).

Demand ↑ (shift right), supply fixed → P*↑ and Q*↑.
Supply ↑, demand fixed → P*↓ and Q*↑.
Both shift: one of P or Q may be ambiguous — say so.

3. Elasticity

PED = (%ΔQd)/(%ΔP)

|PED|>1 elastic; =1 unit; <1 inelastic. Price rise increases TR if inelastic; decreases TR if elastic. Determinants: substitutes, necessity, budget share, time.

P: 10→12 (+20%); Qd: 200→160 (−20%) → PED = −1 → unit elastic; TR unchanged (10×200=2000; 12×160=1920 ≈ check midpoint method if required).

4. Costs, profit max, market structure

Profit max: produce where MR = MC. Competitive firm: P = MR. Monopoly: MR < P; produce MR=MC, read P from demand.

5. Macro: AD–AS and policy

AD = C + I + G + (X − M)

AD↑ (e.g. higher G) → short run: higher Y and higher P along SRAS. LRAS at potential output in classical long-run benchmark.

Monetary policy: interest rates / money (Bank Negara). Lower i → I and durable C↑ → AD↑.
Fiscal policy: G and taxes. Expansionary: ↑G or ↓T → AD↑. Lags and crowding-out may limit impact.
