ECM1953 – Principles of Economics

Degree-level: micro foundations, elasticity applications, market structure, AD-AS policy analysis

1. Scarcity, Choice and Opportunity Cost

Opportunity cost is the value of the next-best alternative forgone when a choice is made. It is the central cost concept in economic reasoning — not merely accounting outlay.

Production possibility frontiers illustrate trade-offs and efficiency; points inside are inefficient, on the frontier efficient given resources/technology, outside unattainable without growth or trade.

2. Demand, Supply and Market Equilibrium

Law of demand: inverse relationship between price and quantity demanded, ceteris paribus. Law of supply: positive relationship between price and quantity supplied, ceteris paribus. Equilibrium equates Qd and Qs. Own-price changes cause movements along curves; non-price determinants shift curves (income, tastes, related-goods prices, expectations, input costs, technology, taxes/subsidies, number of buyers/sellers).

Exam skill: correctly identify shift vs movement, direction of shift, and resulting change in equilibrium P and Q (including ambiguous cases when both curves shift).

3. Elasticity — Computation and Revenue Logic

PED = (%ΔQd) / (%ΔP)

|PED| > 1 elastic; = 1 unit; < 1 inelastic. For a price rise, total revenue rises if demand is inelastic and falls if elastic. Determinants of PED include substitutes, necessity vs luxury, budget share, time horizon.

4. Costs, Profit Maximisation and Market Structure

Short-run cost curves: FC, VC, TC, ATC, AVC, MC. Profit maximisation: produce where MR = MC (subject to shutdown rules comparing price to AVC in the short run for competitive firms).

5. Macro: AD–AS and Policy

AD = C + I + G + (X − M)

Short-run: sticky prices/wages allow output to deviate from potential; AD shifts change Y and P along SRAS. Long-run: output tends toward potential output; AD shifts primarily affect the price level once expectations adjust (depending on model variant taught).

Monetary policy: interest rates / money supply (Bank Negara). Fiscal policy: G and taxation. Expansionary policy aims to raise AD when there is spare capacity; trade-offs include inflation and, for fiscal expansion, financing/crowding-out concerns at intermediate level.

Exam technique: Draw fully labelled diagrams. State ceteris paribus. For policy questions, specify the transmission (e.g. ↓i → ↑I → ↑AD). Distinguish short-run and long-run effects where the syllabus requires it.
