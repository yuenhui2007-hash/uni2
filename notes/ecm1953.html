ECM1953 – Principles of Economics

Microeconomics + Macroeconomics fundamentals for business decision-making.

1. Unit Overview

Covers both micro and macro. Micro focuses on individual decision-making, markets and firm behaviour. Macro examines the economy as a whole – growth, inflation, unemployment and government policy.

2. Key Definitions (Micro)

Scarcity
Unlimited wants and limited resources → choices must be made.
Opportunity Cost
The value of the next best alternative forgone when a choice is made.
Demand
The quantity of a good that consumers are willing and able to buy at various prices, ceteris paribus.
Supply
The quantity of a good that producers are willing and able to sell at various prices, ceteris paribus.
Elasticity
Measure of responsiveness of quantity demanded (or supplied) to a change in price, income or other factors.
Market Structures
Perfect competition, monopolistic competition, oligopoly, monopoly – differ by number of firms, product differentiation, barriers to entry and pricing power.

3. Core Concepts – Microeconomics

Demand & Supply

  • Law of demand: price ↑ → quantity demanded ↓ (inverse relationship).
  • Law of supply: price ↑ → quantity supplied ↑ (positive relationship).
  • Equilibrium: where quantity demanded = quantity supplied.
  • Shifts vs movements: price change = movement along curve; other factors = shift of curve.

Price Elasticity of Demand (PED)

PED = (% change in Qd) / (% change in P)

|PED| > 1 elastic; |PED| < 1 inelastic; |PED| = 1 unit elastic.

Costs of Production

  • Fixed costs (FC) – do not change with output
  • Variable costs (VC) – change with output
  • Total cost (TC) = FC + VC
  • Average total cost (ATC) = TC / Q
  • Marginal cost (MC) = ΔTC / ΔQ

Profit Maximisation

Profit maximised where MR = MC

4. Key Definitions & Concepts – Macroeconomics

GDP (Gross Domestic Product)
Market value of all final goods and services produced within a country in a given period.
Inflation
Sustained increase in the general price level. Measured by CPI or GDP deflator.
Unemployment Rate
Percentage of the labour force that is unemployed and actively seeking work.
Monetary Policy
Central bank actions (interest rates, money supply) to influence the economy.
Fiscal Policy
Government spending and taxation decisions to influence aggregate demand.

Aggregate Demand – Aggregate Supply (AD-AS)

AD = C + I + G + (X – M). Shifts in AD or AS change equilibrium output and price level.

5. Worked Examples

PED Calculation
Price rises from RM10 to RM12 (20% increase). Quantity demanded falls from 100 to 80 (20% decrease).
PED = –20% / 20% = –1 (unit elastic)
Market Structure Comparison
Perfect competition: many firms, identical products, price takers, free entry.
Monopoly: one firm, unique product, price maker, high barriers.

6. Exam Tips

  • Always draw and label diagrams clearly (demand/supply, cost curves, AD-AS).
  • State ceteris paribus when discussing single relationships.
  • Distinguish movements along a curve from shifts of the curve.
  • For policy questions, explain the transmission mechanism (how the policy affects AD/AS).
  • Know the difference between real and nominal variables.
