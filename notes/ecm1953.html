ECM1953 – Principles of Economics

Detailed notes: Micro (demand, supply, elasticity, market structures) + Macro (AD-AS, inflation, unemployment, policy).

1. Microeconomics Core

Scarcity, Choice & Opportunity Cost

Opportunity Cost – the value of the next best alternative forgone when a choice is made.

Demand & Supply

  • Law of Demand: price ↑ → quantity demanded ↓ (inverse)
  • Law of Supply: price ↑ → quantity supplied ↑ (positive)
  • Equilibrium: Qd = Qs
  • Price change → movement along the curve
  • Other factors (income, tastes, input costs, technology, etc.) → shift of the curve

Elasticity

PED = (% change in Qd) / (% change in P)

|PED| > 1 elastic; |PED| < 1 inelastic; |PED| = 1 unit elastic. Revenue rises when price increases if demand is inelastic.

Costs & Profit Maximisation

  • FC, VC, TC, ATC, AVC, MC
  • Profit maximised where MR = MC

Market Structures

  • Perfect competition – many firms, identical product, price taker, free entry
  • Monopolistic competition – many firms, differentiated product, some pricing power
  • Oligopoly – few firms, strategic interdependence
  • Monopoly – one firm, unique product, high barriers, price maker

2. Macroeconomics Core

Key Indicators

  • GDP – market value of final goods and services produced within a country in a period
  • Inflation – sustained rise in general price level (CPI, GDP deflator)
  • Unemployment rate – % of labour force that is unemployed and actively seeking work

Aggregate Demand – Aggregate Supply (AD-AS)

AD = C + I + G + (X − M). Shifts in AD or AS change equilibrium output and price level.

Policy

  • Monetary policy – central bank (Bank Negara) influences interest rates and money supply.
  • Fiscal policy – government spending and taxation.

Expansionary policy aims to increase AD (lower rates, higher G, lower taxes). Contractionary policy aims to reduce inflationary pressure.

3. Exam Tips

  • Always draw and fully label diagrams (axes, curves, equilibrium points, shifts).
  • Distinguish movements along a curve from shifts of the curve.
  • State ceteris paribus when discussing single relationships.
  • For policy questions, explain the transmission mechanism.
  • Know real vs nominal variables.
