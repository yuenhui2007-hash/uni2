ECM1953 – Principles of Economics

Advanced notes: constrained optimisation intuition, comparative statics, market power, policy transmission

1. Core Method

Economics analyses constrained choice. Opportunity cost, marginal analysis (MB = MC at interior optimum), and equilibrium comparative statics are the toolkit. Diagrams are analytical arguments, not decoration — label intercepts, curves, equilibria, and the shock.

2. Demand–Supply Comparative Statics

Own-price movements along curves vs shifts from exogenous determinants. Simultaneous shifts: direction of P or Q may be theoretically ambiguous without magnitude assumptions — state the ambiguity explicitly for marks. Incidence of tax: wedge between buyer and seller prices; burden shares depend on elasticities (more inelastic side bears more).

3. Elasticity and Pricing

PED = %ΔQd / %ΔP   |   Midpoint method when required by the question

TR test: raise price → TR↑ iff demand inelastic. MR for linear demand is twice as steep as AR; monopoly produces where MR = MC and reads price from demand, generating DWL relative to efficient benchmark under standard assumptions.

4. Cost Curves and Structure

SMC intersects AVC and ATC at their minima. Shutdown: P < min AVC in short run. Entry/exit drive long-run competitive equilibrium toward P = min LRAC (zero economic profit) under free entry and identical firms. Oligopoly: strategic interdependence (reaction functions / kinked demand / game matrix as taught). Monopoly: barriers, efficiency critique, regulation rationale.

5. Macro AD–AS and Policy

AD ≡ C+I+G+NX

SRAS upward-sloping under sticky wages/prices or misperceptions; LRAS vertical at potential output in classical benchmark. Demand shock: short-run Y and P move together; long-run adjustment via expectations/wages returns Y to potential with permanent P change (model-dependent narrative — follow your lecturer’s version).

Monetary transmission: i → I, C durables, exchange rate → NX. Fiscal: multiplier diluted by crowding-out and marginal propensity to import; automatic stabilisers vs discretionary policy lags.

Exam: state ceteris paribus; show shock → curve shift → new equilibrium; distinguish movement along vs shift; for policy, write the transmission chain.
