Papers

ACW1120 — Mid-semester style ~60 min · 3 questions

Pattern: theory + T-accounts/adjustments · time tight · show workings · traps on Cash in adjustments
Q1 · Framework 12 marks

(a) Define relevance and faithful representation using Conceptual Framework language. (6)

(b) Explain materiality and give one size-based and one nature-based example. (4)

(c) Why can enhancing characteristics not replace missing relevance? (2)

(a) Relevance: information capable of making a difference to decisions of primary users (predictive and/or confirmatory). Faithful representation: substance of economic phenomena; complete, neutral, free from error as far as practicable.
(b) Materiality = entity-specific relevance; omit/misstate/obscure could reasonably influence users. Size: large inventory error. Nature: related-party fraud even if small $.
(c) Enhancing (comparability, verifiability, timeliness, understandability) improve usefulness only if fundamentals exist.
Q2 · Adjustments 20 marks

Year-end 31 Dec. Prepare adjusting journals with narrations:

  • Prepaid insurance RM9,600 paid 1 Sep for 12 months (recorded as prepaid)
  • Unearned fees RM6,000; by 31 Dec 2/3 earned
  • Equipment cost RM50,000; residual 5,000; life 5 years; SL; full year
  • Accrued wages RM1,800
Ins exp 3,200 / Prepaid 3,200 (4/12×9,600)
Unearned 4,000 / Fees revenue 4,000 (2/3×6,000)
Dep exp 9,000 / AccDep 9,000 ((50k−5k)/5)
Wages exp 1,800 / Wages payable 1,800
Trap: no Cash in any pure adjusting entry.
Q3 · Mini statements impact 18 marks

Before adjustments: revenue 40,000; expenses 22,000; capital 25,000; drawings 2,000. Using Q2 adjustments (ignore inventory), compute adjusted profit and closing equity. Show workings.

Revenue 40,000+4,000=44,000
Expenses 22,000+3,200+9,000+1,800=36,000
Profit=8,000
Closing equity=25,000+8,000−2,000=31,000

ACW1120 — Final style ~2h · multi-part · ~50% unit · hurdle often applies

Pattern: several large questions spanning cycle, inventory, statements, interpretation
Q1 · Periodic inventory 15 marks

Opening inv 18,000; Purchases 95,000; Purchase returns 3,000; discounts 1,500; freight-in 2,200; Closing inv 16,500; Sales 160,000; sales returns 5,000; sales discounts 2,000. Compute net sales, net purchases, COGS, gross profit and GP margin %.

Net sales=160k−5k−2k=153,000
Net purchases=95k−3k−1.5k+2.2k=92,700
COGS=18k+92.7k−16.5k=94,200
GP=153k−94.2k=58,800 · margin=58,800/153,000=38.4%
Q2 · Allowance method 12 marks

AR 60,000; allowance already Cr 800; estimate 4% of AR uncollectible. (a) Adjusting entry (b) net receivables (c) write-off of 500 later — journal.

Required allow=2,400; adj=2,400−800=1,600 → Dr Bad debt exp 1,600 / Cr Allow 1,600
Net AR=60,000−2,400=57,600
Write-off: Dr Allow 500 / Cr AR 500 (no expense)
Q3 · Bank recon logic 10 marks

Explain which side (bank vs books) each item adjusts, and which need journals: deposit in transit; outstanding cheque; NSF cheque; bank collection of note; bank fee.

Bank side only (no journal): deposits in transit, outstanding cheques.
Books side (journals): NSF (Dr AR Cr Cash), bank fee (Dr exp Cr Cash), note collection (Dr Cash Cr Notes/rev as taught).

ACW2220 — Final style decision paper multi-part · show CM first

Pattern: cost behaviour → CVP → special order / keep-drop · assumptions · decision sentence
Q1 · High-low + CVP 18 marks

High 12,000 units cost 110,000; low 7,000 units cost 80,000. SP 25. FC from high-low. (a) VC/unit and FC (b) BE units (c) units for target profit 40,000.

VC=(110k−80k)/(12k−7k)=30k/5k=RM6
FC=110k−6×12k=38,000
CM/u=25−6=19 · BE=38,000/19=2,000 units
Target=(38k+40k)/19=4,105.26 → 4,106 units (or leave exact)
Q2 · Special order full capacity 16 marks

Capacity 5,000; current sales 5,000; SP 40; VC 22; order 800 units at 30; no extra FC. Accept?

CM order=(30−22)×800=6,400
CM lost=(40−22)×800=14,400
Net=6,400−14,400=−8,000 → REJECT
State qualitative caveat (customer relationship) if asked.
Q3 · Keep or drop 14 marks

Segment CM 70,000; avoidable FC 45,000; allocated common FC 20,000 continues. Alternative use of space CM 18,000. Drop?

Effect of drop= −70,000 +45,000 +18,000 = −7,000 → KEEP
Common allocated FC irrelevant.

BFW1001 — Calculation + decision paper

Pattern: formula first · timeline · Accept/Reject · NPV vs IRR ranking
Q1 · TVM 12 marks

PV of RM8,000 annuity end of year for 6 years at 9%. Then restate if annuity due.

Factor=[1−1.09−6]/0.09≈4.4859
PV_ord=8,000×4.4859=35,887
PV_due=35,887×1.09=39,117
Q2 · NPV 16 marks

I₀=100,000; CF 30k, 35k, 40k, 25k; r=10%. Compute NPV and decide.

PV≈27,273+28,926+30,053+17,075=103,327
NPV≈+3,327 → ACCEPT
Q3 · Ranking conflict 12 marks

Project S: IRR 28%, NPV 2,000. Project L: IRR 18%, NPV 9,000. Mutually exclusive, same risk. Which and why?

Choose L — NPV measures value added to shareholders; IRR% can favour small projects (scale problem).

BTW1042 — IRAC case paper

Pattern: every answer structured Issue · Rule · Application · Conclusion
Q1 · Offer vs ITT 15 marks

Shop displays a laptop tagged RM2,499. Customer hands card at counter; staff says price error, true price RM3,299. Using IRAC, is there a contract at 2,499?

I: Contract at tagged price?
R: Display usually invitation to treat; offer at cashier; acceptance by seller.
A: Tag is ITT; customer’s presentation is offer; staff rejects → no acceptance at 2,499.
C: No contract at RM2,499 (classic display rule; watch for clear unilateral offer wording).
Q2 · Negligence 20 marks

Tax agent carelessly files wrong form; client assessed extra tax and penalties. IRAC on negligence liability.

I: Liable in negligence?
R: Duty (professional relationship), breach (competent agent standard), causation, remoteness of pure economic loss.
A: Duty clear; filing error below standard; loss flows from breach; type of loss foreseeable for tax work.
C: Likely liable for foreseeable financial loss (subject to any contributory facts).

ECM1953 — Diagram + explain paper

Q1 14 marks

Market for instant noodles. Income rises (normal good) and input costs rise simultaneously. Using a D-S diagram description, what happens to equilibrium P and Q? Which is ambiguous?

D shifts right; S shifts left → P* rises; Q* ambiguous (depends which shift larger). State assumptions.
Q2 12 marks

PED: P 10→12, Qd 200→170. Compute |PED| and effect on TR if firm raises price further in this range.

%ΔP=+20%; %ΔQ=−15%; |PED|=0.75 inelastic → price rise raises TR in this range.

ETW1001 — Formula → number → interpretation

Q1 12 marks

x̄=52, s=8, n=16. Approximate 95% CI for μ (use z=1.96). Interpret.

SE=2; CI=52±3.92 → [48.08, 55.92]
Interpretation: method captures μ in 95% of repeated samples (frequentist); do not say “95% probability μ is in this interval” after observing it.
Q2 12 marks

Ŷ=20+1.5X. Interpret slope. Residual if X=10 and actual Y=40?

Slope: +1 unit X associated with +1.5 Y on average.
Predicted=35; residual=40−35=+5 (under-predicted).

MGW1010 — Essay / critique paper

Q1 25 marks

“Scientific Management is obsolete for all modern organisations.” Discuss with definition, application, and critique.

Define Taylor principles → apply where routine tasks still benefit → critique: ignores social needs, knowledge work, intrinsic motivation → contingency: useful for some processes, not universal → conclude partial obsolescence, not total.
